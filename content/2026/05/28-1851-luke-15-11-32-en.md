For your brother was dead and has come back to life! He was lost, but now he is found!'

## The Story

Jesus shares this famous narrative in response to religious leaders who are scandalized by his choice of dinner companions. In the culture of the time, a younger son demanding his inheritance was tantamount to wishing his father dead. The subsequent descent into tending swine represents the ultimate loss of identity and ritual purity for a Jewish listener. Yet, the father's reaction breaks all social conventions of dignity. An elder of the community would never run, as it required lifting his robes and exposing his legs, bringing public shame. The father absorbs this shame to shield his returning child from the judgment of the village.

## Application for Today

We often view our relationship with the divine as a transactional ledger, assuming our failures must be met with severe punishment or heavy probation. We rehearse our apologies, hoping to earn back a fraction of our former standing. But grace operates on an entirely different economy. Before we can even finish our carefully crafted speeches of repentance, we are met with an undignified, overwhelming embrace. The invitation here is to stop trying to negotiate our way back into the household as hired hands. We are simply asked to walk up the road and let ourselves be welcomed home.
