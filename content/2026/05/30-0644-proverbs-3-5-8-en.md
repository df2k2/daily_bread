## The Reading (NLT)

**Proverbs 3:5-8**

> <sup class="verse-num">5</sup> Trust in the Lord with all your heart; do not depend on your own understanding. <sup class="verse-num">6</sup> Seek his will in all you do, and he will show you which path to take. <sup class="verse-num">7</sup> Don't be impressed with your own wisdom. Instead, fear the Lord and turn away from evil. <sup class="verse-num">8</sup> Then you will have healing for your body and strength for your bones.

## The Story

The book of Proverbs frames wisdom not as the accumulation of facts, but as a posture of the heart. In the ancient Near East, understanding was often tied to self-sufficiency and the ability to outmaneuver life's challenges. Yet the wisdom tradition of Israel subverts this by suggesting that true insight begins with humility. The writer contrasts the heavy burden of self-reliance with the freedom of trusting a higher wisdom. To revere the divine here is not about terror, but about a deep, orienting respect that puts human limitations into proper perspective. When we stop trying to be the ultimate architects of our own lives, we align ourselves with the way the world was actually meant to work.

## Application for Today

We live in a culture that constantly tells us to trust our own instincts and forge our own paths. While self-confidence has its place, the pressure to figure everything out on our own can become an exhausting weight. This passage invites us to lay down the demand of having all the answers. Surrendering our need for control is not a sign of weakness; it is a profound act of trust in a God who sees the wider landscape. When we invite God into our everyday decisions and turn away from the destructive habits that entangle us, we often find a surprising physical and emotional relief. The anxiety that tightens our chests begins to loosen, replaced by a steady, grounding peace.
