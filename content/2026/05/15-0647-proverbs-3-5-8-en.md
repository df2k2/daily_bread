## The Reading (NLT)

**Proverbs 3:5-8**

> <sup class="verse-num">5</sup> Trust in the Lord with all your heart; do not depend on your own understanding. <sup class="verse-num">6</sup> Seek his will in all you do, and he will show you which path to take. <sup class="verse-num">7</sup> Don't be impressed with your own wisdom. Instead, fear the Lord and turn away from evil. <sup class="verse-num">8</sup> Then you will have healing for your body and strength for your bones.

## The Story

The Book of Proverbs often frames wisdom as a transmission from parent to child, meant to prepare the next generation for the complexities of life. In the ancient Near East, wisdom was not just intellectual knowledge but the practical skill of living well. The writer addresses a fundamental human struggle by highlighting the illusion that we can figure everything out on our own. By contrasting total reliance on the Divine with human self-sufficiency, the text establishes that true wisdom begins with humility. It acknowledges that human perspective is inherently limited, no matter how clever we might feel.

## Application for Today

We spend so much energy trying to map out every detail of our lives, analyzing every possible outcome to avoid failure. Yet, this passage invites us to release that exhausting grip on the steering wheel. Acknowledging our limitations is not a sign of weakness; it is the doorway to profound peace. When we invite God into our daily decisions, the pressure to be the sole architect of our destiny begins to lift. The result is not necessarily a life free of obstacles, but rather a deep, sustaining vitality that carries us through them. We can rest in the knowledge that we are being guided by a perspective far wider than our own.
