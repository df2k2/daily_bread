## The Reading (NLT)

**Isaiah 40:28-31**

> <sup class="verse-num">28</sup> Have you never heard? Have you never understood? The Lord is the everlasting God, the Creator of all the earth. He never grows weak or weary. No one can measure the depths of his understanding. <sup class="verse-num">29</sup> He gives power to the weak and strength to the powerless. <sup class="verse-num">30</sup> Even youths will become weak and tired, and young men will fall in exhaustion. <sup class="verse-num">31</sup> But those who trust in the Lord will find new strength. They will soar high on wings like eagles. They will run and not grow weary. They will walk and not faint.

## The Story

The Israelites were in exile in Babylon, feeling forgotten and completely drained. They looked around at the towering monuments of their captors and wondered if their God had lost His power or simply lost interest in them. The prophet speaks into this profound communal exhaustion. He shifts their gaze away from their immediate political reality and points them to the cosmos. He reminds them that the Creator is never bound by human limitations, fatigue, or the rise and fall of empires.

## Application for Today

We live in a culture that prizes endless hustle, where we are expected to push through our limits until we burn out. It is easy to feel as though we are running on empty, relying entirely on our own dwindling reserves. This passage invites us to admit our inherent fragility. Exhaustion isn't a moral failure, but rather a reminder that we are finite creatures in need of an infinite Creator. When we stop striving and start resting in divine presence, we find a different kind of endurance. It is a quiet, steady resilience that carries us forward when our own momentum fails.
