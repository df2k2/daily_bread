## The Reading (NLT)

**1 John 4:7-16**

> Dear friends, let us continue to love one another, for love comes from God. Anyone who loves is a child of God and knows God. But anyone who does not love does not know God, for God is love. God showed how much he loved us by sending his one and only Son into the world so that we might have eternal life through him. This is real love not that we loved God, but that he loved us and sent his Son as a sacrifice to take away our sins. Dear friends, since God loved us that much, we surely ought to love each other. No one has ever seen God. But if we love each other, God lives in us, and his love is brought to full expression in us. And God has given us his Spirit as proof that we live in him and he in us. Furthermore, we have seen with our own eyes and now testify that the Father sent his Son to be the Savior of the world. All who declare that Jesus is the Son of God have God living in them, and they live in God. We know how much God loves us, and we have put our trust in his love. God is love, and all who live in love live in God, and God lives in them.

## The Story

The early Christian communities faced intense internal fractures and theological disputes that threatened to tear them apart. In this letter, John addresses a group of believers who are likely confused about what truly marks a follower of Christ. Instead of offering a complex doctrinal checklist, the author points them to the most fundamental reality of their faith. He grounds the identity of the community not in their own emotional capacity, but in the very character of the Creator. The text insists that the divine nature is entirely defined by self-giving affection, demonstrated historically in the giving of the Son. Therefore, the invisible Creator becomes visible precisely when ordinary people choose to care for one another.

## Application for Today

It is easy to think of love as a feeling we must somehow generate on our own when dealing with difficult people. Yet this passage shifts the burden off our shoulders by reminding us that we are merely conduits, not the source. When we struggle to find patience or grace for someone else, we are invited to tap into the wellspring of the Creator's affection for us. Loving others becomes a response to the grace we have already received rather than a prerequisite for earning divine approval. Our small, daily acts of kindness are actually profound theological statements. By choosing compassion in our homes and workplaces, we are quite literally putting the character of God on display for the world to see.
