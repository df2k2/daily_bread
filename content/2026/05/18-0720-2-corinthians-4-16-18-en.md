## The Reading (NLT)

**2 Corinthians 4:16-18**

> <sup class="verse-num">16</sup> That is why we never give up. Though our bodies are dying, our spirits are being renewed every day. <sup class="verse-num">17</sup> For our present troubles are small and won't last very long. Yet they produce for us a glory that vastly outweighs them and will last forever! <sup class="verse-num">18</sup> So we don't look at the troubles we can see now; rather, we fix our gaze on things that cannot be seen. For the things we see now will soon be gone, but the things we cannot see will last forever.

## The Story

Paul wrote to the church in Corinth during a season of intense personal hardship and physical vulnerability. He was constantly facing persecution, illness, and the natural reality of aging. In the ancient world, physical suffering was often interpreted as a sign of divine disfavor or personal failure. Yet, Paul completely flips this cultural script. He introduces a profound contrast between the outer realities of physical decay and the inner reality of spiritual transformation. Instead of minimizing his pain, he places it on a scale against an unimaginable, enduring future.

## Application for Today

It is incredibly easy to be overwhelmed by the tangible struggles right in front of us. Illness, financial stress, and aging are visible, heavy realities that demand our daily attention. This passage invites us to shift our focal point rather than ignore our pain. By turning our attention to the spiritual work happening quietly beneath the surface, we find a steady anchor. The difficulties of today are real, but they do not have the final word. We are invited to trust that a quiet, enduring restoration is unfolding within us, outlasting everything we can currently touch or measure.
