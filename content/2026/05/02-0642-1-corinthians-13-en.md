## The Reading (NLT)

**1 Corinthians 13**

> <sup class="verse-num">1</sup> If I could speak all the languages of earth and of angels, but didn't love others, I would only be a noisy gong or a clanging cymbal. <sup class="verse-num">2</sup> If I had the gift of prophecy, and if I understood all of God's secret plans and possessed all knowledge, and if I had such faith that I could move mountains, but didn't love others, I would be nothing. <sup class="verse-num">3</sup> If I gave everything I have to the poor and even sacrificed my body, I could boast about it; but if I didn't love others, I would have gained nothing. <sup class="verse-num">4</sup> Love is patient and kind. Love is not jealous or boastful or proud <sup class="verse-num">5</sup> or rude. It does not demand its own way. It is not irritable, and it keeps no record of being wronged. <sup class="verse-num">6</sup> It does not rejoice about injustice but rejoices whenever the truth wins out. <sup class="verse-num">7</sup> Love never gives up, never loses faith, is always hopeful, and endures through every circumstance. <sup class="verse-num">8</sup> Prophecy and speaking in unknown languages and special knowledge will become useless. But love will last forever! <sup class="verse-num">9</sup> Now our knowledge is partial and incomplete, and even the gift of prophecy reveals only part of the whole picture! <sup class="verse-num">10</sup> But when the time of perfection comes, these partial things will become useless. <sup class="verse-num">11</sup> When I was a child, I spoke and thought and reasoned as a child. But when I grew up, I put away childish things. <sup class="verse-num">12</sup> Now we see things imperfectly, like puzzling reflections in a mirror, but then we will see everything with perfect clarity. All that I know now is partial and incomplete, but then I will know everything completely, just as God now knows me completely. <sup class="verse-num">13</sup> Three things will last forever faith, hope, and love and the greatest of these is love.

## The Story

The early community in Corinth was a vibrant but chaotic group, deeply fascinated by spiritual status and spectacular abilities. They valued eloquent speech, profound knowledge, and dramatic displays of faith, often using these gifts to compete with one another. Paul addresses this fractured community by shifting their focus entirely. He points out that even the most impressive spiritual achievements are hollow if they lack genuine care for others. He describes a way of living that strips away ego and self-interest, focusing instead on patience, endurance, and kindness. This was a radical redefinition of value for a culture obsessed with honor and public recognition.

## Application for Today

We often measure our worth by our accomplishments, our intellect, or the visible impact we make in the world. Yet, this letter reminds us that all our greatest successes are temporary and ultimately meaningless without a foundation of deep, self-giving care. True maturity involves moving past the need to be right, to be noticed, or to be the most capable person in the room. Instead, we are invited to cultivate a quiet resilience that bears with others and celebrates truth over personal victory. While our current understanding of life is limited and fragmented, the way we treat one another remains eternally significant. What matters most is not what we achieve, but how deeply we commit to the well-being of those around us.
