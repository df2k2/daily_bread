## The Reading (NLT)

**John 3:16-21**

> <sup class="verse-num">16</sup> For this is how God loved the world: He gave his one and only Son, so that everyone who believes in him will not perish but have eternal life. <sup class="verse-num">17</sup> God sent his Son into the world not to judge the world, but to save the world through him. <sup class="verse-num">18</sup> There is no judgment against anyone who believes in him. But anyone who does not believe in him has already been judged for not believing in God's one and only Son. <sup class="verse-num">19</sup> And the judgment is based on this fact: God's light came into the world, but people loved the darkness more than the light, for their actions were evil. <sup class="verse-num">20</sup> All who do evil hate the light and refuse to go near it for fear their sins will be exposed. <sup class="verse-num">21</sup> But those who do what is right come to the light so others can see that they are doing what God wants.

## The Story

Nicodemus, a religious leader, approaches Jesus under the cover of night, perhaps afraid of what others might think. In this quiet, secretive encounter, Jesus shifts the conversation from rules to rescue. He explains that the divine purpose is not condemnation, but an expansive, world-altering love. The imagery shifts to a stark contrast between light and shadow. The tragedy described is not just about failing a moral test, but actively hiding from the very light meant to bring healing. It is a profound picture of human nature and our tendency to conceal our brokenness rather than allowing it to be illuminated.

## Application for Today

We often carry an underlying fear that if God truly saw us, the response would be harsh judgment. We hide our mistakes, our doubts, and our deepest flaws in the dark, convinced that exposure means rejection. Yet this passage flips that assumption entirely. The light is offered as a sanctuary, not an interrogation lamp. Stepping into it means trusting that love is the final word, not condemnation. When we allow our true selves to be seen, we do not find a judge waiting to punish us, but a rescuer ready to make us whole. We are invited to stop hiding and let the warmth of that love reach the places we usually keep locked away.
