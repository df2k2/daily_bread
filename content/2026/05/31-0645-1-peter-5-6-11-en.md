## The Reading (NLT)

**1 Peter 5:6-11**

> <sup class="verse-num">6</sup> So humble yourselves under the mighty power of God, and at the right time he will lift you up in honor. <sup class="verse-num">7</sup> Give all your worries and cares to God, for he cares about you. <sup class="verse-num">8</sup> Stay alert! Watch out for your great enemy, the devil. He prowls around like a roaring lion, looking for someone to devour. <sup class="verse-num">9</sup> Stand firm against him, and be strong in your faith. Remember that your family of believers all over the world is going through the same kind of suffering you are. <sup class="verse-num">10</sup> In his kindness God called you to share in his eternal glory by means of Christ Jesus. So after you have suffered a little while, he will restore, support, and strengthen you, and he will place you on a firm foundation. <sup class="verse-num">11</sup> All power to him forever! Amen.

## The Story

The early Christian communities scattered across Asia Minor faced intense social alienation and sporadic persecution. The writer of this letter acknowledges the visceral reality of their fear, comparing the spiritual and physical threats they face to a stalking predator. Yet, instead of promising an immediate escape route, the text calls for spiritual grounding and a broader perspective. The readers are reminded they are not isolated in their struggles; a vast, unseen community shares their exact burdens. The passage frames their current suffering as a temporary season, pointing toward a divine promise of ultimate rebuilding and stability.

## Application for Today

It is easy to feel entirely alone when anxiety or hardship strikes. We often carry our worries as if we have to solve every crisis on our own, making us vulnerable to despair. This passage invites us to release that exhausting grip and hand our fears over to a God who actively cares for our well-being. Being vigilant does not mean living in constant paranoia; it means recognizing the destructive forces that try to consume our peace. When we remember that hardship is a shared human experience, the isolation of our struggles begins to fade. We can trust that God is at work, ready to restore our strength and set our feet back on solid ground.
