## The Reading (NLT)

## The Story

In ancient Judea, vineyards were a familiar sight, carefully tended to ensure a rich harvest. During his final meal with his closest friends, Jesus draws on this everyday imagery to explain their ongoing relationship. He describes a living, organic connection where he is the central stalk and they are the offshoots. The agricultural process he mentions involves careful, sometimes painful, trimming by a master gardener to promote health and vitality. Rather than focusing on independent effort, the emphasis rests entirely on staying attached. The ultimate goal of this connection is not just survival, but an expansive, deep-rooted joy.

## Application for Today

We often treat our spiritual lives as a series of tasks to accomplish or moral hurdles to clear. Yet this agrarian picture invites us into a totally different way of living. Growth does not happen through sheer willpower or frantic activity, but through intentional resting in our source of life. When we experience seasons of loss or reduction, it may actually be a careful shaping meant to bring about deeper maturity. Our primary responsibility is simply to maintain the connection. By making our home in that sustaining love, we find the strength to thrive and the capacity to experience genuine gladness.
