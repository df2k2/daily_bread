## The Reading (NLT)

**John 3:16-21**

> For this is how God loved the world: He gave his one and only Son, so that everyone who believes in him will not perish but have eternal life. God sent his Son into the world not to judge the world, but to save the world through him. There is no judgment against anyone who believes in him. But anyone who does not believe in him has already been judged for not believing in God's one and only Son. And the judgment is based on this fact: God's light came into the world, but people loved the darkness more than the light, for their actions were evil. All who do evil hate the light and refuse to go near it for fear their sins will be exposed. But those who do what is right come to the light so others can see that they are doing what God wants.

## The Story

In the ancient world, darkness offered a cloak for things people wanted to keep hidden. When this Gospel was written, the contrast between light and shadow served as a powerful metaphor for truth and deception. The author emphasizes that the divine intention was never to condemn humanity, but to rescue it. The arrival of the divine is described as an illumination that reveals reality as it truly is. Yet, the text acknowledges a tragic human tendency to prefer the familiar comfort of shadows over the exposing glare of truth.

## Application for Today

It is easy to hide the broken or shameful parts of our lives, fearing that if they are seen, we will be rejected. This passage flips that fear on its head by assuring us that the light is meant for our rescue, not our ruin. When we bring our authentic selves out of the shadows, we find a love that is vast enough to hold all our imperfections. Healing begins the moment we stop pretending and allow ourselves to be fully known. Instead of running from the light, we can let it guide us toward a life of honesty and peace. The invitation is simply to step forward and trust the grace waiting for us.
