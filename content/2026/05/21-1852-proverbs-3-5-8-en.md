## The Reading (NLT)

**Proverbs 3:5-8**

> <sup class="verse-num">5</sup> Trust in the Lord with all your heart; do not depend on your own understanding. <sup class="verse-num">6</sup> Seek his will in all you do, and he will show you which path to take. <sup class="verse-num">7</sup> Don't be impressed with your own wisdom. Instead, fear the Lord and turn away from evil. <sup class="verse-num">8</sup> Then you will have healing for your body and strength for your bones.

## The Story

The book of Proverbs frequently frames wisdom as a path or a journey, contrasting the way of the wise with the way of the foolish. In the ancient Near East, survival often depended on deep communal knowledge rather than isolated individual cleverness. The writer of this section acts as a parent instructing a child, warning them against the illusion of self-sufficiency. Human intellect, while a gift, is inherently limited by our narrow vantage point in time and space. Ancient readers understood that relying solely on what they could see or deduce was a fragile strategy for a flourishing life. True wisdom meant anchoring oneself to a wider, eternal perspective.

## Application for Today

We live in an era that highly prizes individual expertise and self-reliance, making it easy to believe we can think our way out of any problem. Yet, when we hit the boundaries of our own logic, we often find ourselves exhausted and anxious. This passage invites us to surrender the heavy burden of having to figure everything out on our own. By acknowledging our limitations, we make room for divine guidance to shape our decisions. Humility becomes the starting point for genuine healing, relieving the physical and spiritual tension of trying to control the uncontrollable. We are invited to step forward with open hands, allowing a greater wisdom to map the road ahead.
