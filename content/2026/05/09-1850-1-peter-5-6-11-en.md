## The Reading (NLT)

**1 Peter 5:6-11**

> So humble yourselves under the mighty power of God, and at the right time he will lift you up in honor. Give all your worries and cares to God, for he cares about you. Stay alert! Watch out for your great enemy, the devil. He prowls around like a roaring lion, looking for someone to devour. Stand firm against him, and be strong in your faith. Remember that your family of believers all over the world is going through the same kind of suffering you are. In his kindness God called you to share in his eternal glory by means of Christ Jesus. So after you have suffered a little while, he will restore, support, and strengthen you, and he will place you on a firm foundation. All power to him forever! Amen.

## The Story

The early Christians receiving this letter were scattered across Asia Minor, enduring severe social alienation and hostility for their faith. They likely felt isolated, wondering if their quiet suffering was unnoticed or if they were the only ones facing such intense pushback. The author writes to remind them of a profound reality: their struggles are shared by a global community of believers. Instead of fighting for their own immediate vindication, they are invited to yield to God's broader timeline. They are warned that spiritual opposition thrives on fear and isolation, seeking to consume those who lose their footing. Yet, the overarching promise is one of divine attention and eventual restoration.

## Application for Today

It is remarkably easy to let anxiety convince us that we are entirely alone in our struggles. When we face difficulties, our instinct is often to carry the weight ourselves, which only makes us more vulnerable to despair and spiritual exhaustion. This passage offers a gentle but firm corrective, reminding us that God is actively attentive to our worries and invites us to hand them over completely. We are called to stay clear-headed and resilient, recognizing that our hardships are part of a shared human and spiritual experience. The hope we hold is that the pain of the present is not the final word. God is at work to rebuild our strength, steady our steps, and place us on ground that will not give way.
