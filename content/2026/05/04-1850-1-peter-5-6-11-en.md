## The Reading (NLT)

**1 Peter 5:6-11**

> So humble yourselves under the mighty power of God, and at the right time he will lift you up in honor. Give all your worries and cares to God, for he cares about you. Stay alert! Watch out for your great enemy, the devil. He prowls around like a roaring lion, looking for someone to devour. Stand firm against him, and be strong in your faith. Remember that your family of believers all over the world is going through the same kind of suffering you are. In his kindness God called you to share in his eternal glory by means of Christ Jesus. So after you have suffered a little while, he will restore, support, and strengthen you, and he will place you on a firm foundation. All power to him forever! Amen.

## The Story

The early Christians receiving this letter were scattered across Asia Minor, facing intense social pressure and the looming threat of persecution. They were likely exhausted, anxious, and feeling profoundly isolated in their struggles. The author addresses this reality head-on, acknowledging the visceral fear that can accompany suffering. He uses the vivid imagery of a predator circling in the dark to describe the spiritual opposition they face. Yet, he reminds them that their experience is not unique, connecting their local pain to a global community of believers enduring similar hardships. The focus is shifted from their immediate vulnerability to the enduring strength of the one holding them.

## Application for Today

It is easy to feel entirely alone when anxiety and hardship close in around us. Our worries often act like a heavy weight, pulling our gaze downward and isolating us from those who might understand. This passage invites us to consciously release that grip, handing our deepest fears over to a God who is intimately invested in our well-being. We are called to remain watchful, recognizing that despair and isolation are forces that seek to consume our hope. When we remember that our struggles are part of a shared human experience, the burden becomes a little lighter. We can trust that the current season of pain is not the final chapter, but a prelude to deep, lasting restoration.
