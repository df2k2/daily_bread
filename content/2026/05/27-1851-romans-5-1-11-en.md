## The Reading (NLT)

**Romans 5:1-11**

> <sup class="verse-num">1</sup> Therefore, since we have been made right in God's sight by faith, we have peace with God because of what Jesus Christ our Lord has done for us. <sup class="verse-num">2</sup> Because of our faith, Christ has brought us into this place of undeserved privilege where we now stand, and we confidently and joyfully look forward to sharing God's glory. <sup class="verse-num">3</sup> We can rejoice, too, when we run into problems and trials, for we know that they help us develop endurance. <sup class="verse-num">4</sup> And endurance develops strength of character, and character strengthens our confident hope of salvation. <sup class="verse-num">5</sup> And this hope will not lead to disappointment. For we know how dearly God loves us, because he has given us the Holy Spirit to fill our hearts with his love. <sup class="verse-num">6</sup> When we were utterly helpless, Christ came at just the right time and died for us sinners. <sup class="verse-num">7</sup> Now, most people would not be willing to die for an upright person, though someone might perhaps be willing to die for a person who is especially good. <sup class="verse-num">8</sup> But God showed his great love for us by sending Christ to die for us while we were still sinners. <sup class="verse-num">9</sup> And since we have been made right in God's sight by the blood of Christ, he will certainly save us from God's condemnation. <sup class="verse-num">10</sup> For since our friendship with God was restored by the death of his Son while we were still his enemies, we will certainly be saved through the life of his Son. <sup class="verse-num">11</sup> So now we can rejoice in our wonderful new relationship with God because our Lord Jesus Christ has made us friends of God.

## The Story

Paul writes to a divided and anxious community in Rome, outlining the profound reality of their new standing with the divine. He describes a sequence where hardship is not a sign of abandonment, but a crucible for spiritual maturity. In his view, the difficulties of life act as a catalyst, forging endurance, which in turn shapes a resilient character. This progression ultimately blossoms into a steadfast hope. To anchor this bold claim, Paul points to a historical reality: the ultimate act of love occurred when humanity was at its absolute weakest and most undeserving. The foundation of their peace is not their own moral perfection, but a reconciliation initiated entirely by grace.

## Application for Today

We often assume that peace requires us to first get our lives in perfect order. Yet this passage reminds us that grace meets us precisely at the point of our deepest vulnerability and failure. When we face trials, it is easy to feel as though we have been left to fend for ourselves. Instead, these moments can become the very soil where genuine endurance takes root, leading to a hope that remains unshaken by circumstances. We do not have to manufacture this resilience on our own. We can rest in the quiet assurance that we are already held, loved, and fully reconciled by a love that stepped in when we were entirely helpless.
