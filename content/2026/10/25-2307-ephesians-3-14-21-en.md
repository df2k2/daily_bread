## The Reading (NLT)

**Ephesians 3:14-21**

> <sup class="verse-num">14</sup> When I think of all this, I fall to my knees and pray to the Father, <sup class="verse-num">15</sup> the Creator of everything in heaven and on earth. <sup class="verse-num">16</sup> I pray that from his glorious, unlimited resources he will empower you with inner strength through his Spirit. <sup class="verse-num">17</sup> Then Christ will make his home in your hearts as you trust in him. Your roots will grow down into God's love and keep you strong. <sup class="verse-num">18</sup> And may you have the power to understand, as all God's people should, how wide, how long, how high, and how deep his love is. <sup class="verse-num">19</sup> May you experience the love of Christ, though it is too great to understand fully. Then you will be made complete with all the fullness of life and power that comes from God. <sup class="verse-num">20</sup> Now all glory to God, who is able, through his mighty power at work within us, to accomplish infinitely more than we might ask or think. <sup class="verse-num">21</sup> Glory to him in the church and in Christ Jesus through all generations forever and ever! Amen.

## The Story

The author of this letter is writing from a place of profound physical limitation, likely a Roman prison cell. Stripped of his freedom and separated from the communities he helped build, he might easily have succumbed to despair. Instead, his focus shifts from his external confinement to the boundless interior landscape of the human heart. He offers a soaring prayer for his readers, asking that their inner lives be fortified by a divine presence. The language he uses is almost architectural, speaking of roots growing deep and foundations being built on an unimaginably vast love. He wants them to grasp dimensions of affection and grace that stretch far beyond human logic.

## Application for Today

When our external world feels fragile or restrictive, we often look for escape routes or quick solutions. Yet this passage invites us to seek expansion inward rather than outward. We are encouraged to build our lives on a foundation of love that is wide enough to hold our grief, deep enough to reach our lowest moments, and high enough to lift our perspective. This kind of spiritual grounding does not immediately change our circumstances, but it fundamentally changes us within them. As we trust in this stabilizing presence, we discover a quiet resilience taking root in our lives. We realize that the grace at work within us is far more expansive than the boundaries of our current struggles.
