## The Reading (NLT)

**John 3:16-21**

> <sup class="verse-num">16</sup> For this is how God loved the world: He gave his one and only Son, so that everyone who believes in him will not perish but have eternal life. <sup class="verse-num">17</sup> God sent his Son into the world not to judge the world, but to save the world through him. <sup class="verse-num">18</sup> There is no judgment against anyone who believes in him. But anyone who does not believe in him has already been judged for not believing in God's one and only Son. <sup class="verse-num">19</sup> And the judgment is based on this fact: God's light came into the world, but people loved the darkness more than the light, for their actions were evil. <sup class="verse-num">20</sup> All who do evil hate the light and refuse to go near it for fear their sins will be exposed. <sup class="verse-num">21</sup> But those who do what is right come to the light so others can see that they are doing what God wants.

## The Story

Nicodemus comes to his midnight meeting seeking answers without drawing attention. In this quiet, shadow-filled encounter, the conversation reveals a divine rescue mission rooted entirely in love rather than condemnation. The narrative contrasts the safety people feel in darkness with the illuminating presence of the divine. It suggests that hiding our flaws is a natural human reflex, rooted in the fear of exposure and judgment. Yet, the divine intention is not to shame the world but to offer a path out of the shadows. The light arrives as a beacon of rescue, inviting humanity to step out of hiding.

## Application for Today

It is easy to assume that if our true selves were fully seen, we would be met with harsh judgment. We often retreat into our own versions of darkness, keeping our struggles, fears, and failures hidden from others and from God. But the divine invitation flips this expectation completely upside down. The light is offered not as a harsh spotlight to expose and humiliate, but as the warmth of a morning sun bringing healing. Stepping into that light means trusting that we are loved enough to be rescued, not condemned. When we allow ourselves to be truly seen, we find that the grace waiting for us is far greater than our fear. We can walk forward in truth, knowing that the work being done in us is guided by love.
