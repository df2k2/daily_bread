## The Reading (NLT)

**Colossians 3:12-17**

> <sup class="verse-num">12</sup> Since God chose you to be the holy people he loves, you must clothe yourselves with tenderhearted mercy, kindness, humility, gentleness, and patience. <sup class="verse-num">13</sup> Make allowance for each other's faults, and forgive anyone who offends you. Remember, the Lord forgave you, so you must forgive others. <sup class="verse-num">14</sup> Above all, clothe yourselves with love, which binds us all together in perfect harmony. <sup class="verse-num">15</sup> And let the peace that comes from Christ rule in your hearts. For as members of one body you are called to live in peace. And always be thankful. <sup class="verse-num">16</sup> Let the message about Christ, in all its richness, fill your lives. Teach and counsel each other with all the wisdom he gives. Sing psalms and hymns and spiritual songs to God with thankful hearts. <sup class="verse-num">17</sup> And whatever you do or say, do it as a representative of the Lord Jesus, giving thanks through him to God the Father.

## The Story

Paul writes to the Colossian church from a Roman prison, eager to help them understand how their new identity in Christ changes their everyday interactions. He relies on a powerful metaphor of getting dressed, urging these early believers to consciously adopt virtues just as they would a heavy winter cloak. This is not merely a checklist for individual moral achievement, but a practical survival guide for a diverse and messy group of people learning to live together. By naming love as the outermost garment that binds everything else in harmony, he emphasizes that unity requires deliberate, daily effort. He envisions a local church where peace acts as the umpire settling disputes, and where gratitude sets the rhythm for their shared life.

## Application for Today

We wake up every morning and choose what to wear, but we rarely give the same deliberate thought to our emotional and spiritual posture. This letter challenges us to intentionally wrap ourselves in humility and patience before we step out the door to face the world. When friction inevitably arises in our relationships, we are invited to let peace be the deciding factor rather than our desperate need to be right. Forgiveness is framed not as a fleeting feeling, but as an active choice modeled after the grace we have already been given. As we navigate our ordinary responsibilities, letting thankfulness shape our words transforms mundane tasks into quiet acts of worship. Love remains the essential thread that holds our fractured lives and communities together.
