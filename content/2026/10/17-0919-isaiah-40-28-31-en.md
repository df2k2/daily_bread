## The Reading (NLT)

**Isaiah 40:28-31**

> <sup class="verse-num">28</sup> Have you never heard? Have you never understood? The Lord is the everlasting God, the Creator of all the earth. He never grows weak or weary. No one can measure the depths of his understanding. <sup class="verse-num">29</sup> He gives power to the weak and strength to the powerless. <sup class="verse-num">30</sup> Even youths will become weak and tired, and young men will fall in exhaustion. <sup class="verse-num">31</sup> But those who trust in the Lord will find new strength. They will soar high on wings like eagles. They will run and not grow weary. They will walk and not faint.

## The Story

The prophet speaks to a community in exile, a people who feel forgotten by their Creator and entirely drained of hope. They have been stripped of their homeland and their institutions, leaving them vulnerable and emotionally spent. In this season of devastation, the writer points them away from their own depleted reserves and toward the infinite capacity of the Divine. The ancient audience is reminded that the architect of the cosmos is immune to fatigue and burnout. While human vigor always has a hard limit, no matter how youthful or resilient a person might be, divine energy is boundless. True vitality is not self-generated, but rather absorbed by those who choose to rest in God's presence.

## Application for Today

We live in a culture that prizes endless productivity and glorifies the hustle, convincing us that we must generate our own momentum. When we inevitably hit the wall of our own limitations, we often feel a sense of failure or shame. This passage offers a profound relief by reminding us that we were never designed to run on our own exhaustible fuel. Acknowledging our exhaustion is not a spiritual defect, but the very prerequisite for receiving divine strength. By pausing to trust in the eternal rhythm of God, we open ourselves to a power that sustains us through the long haul. We learn that endurance isn't about pushing harder, but about resting in the One who never tires.
