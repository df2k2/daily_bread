## The Reading (NLT)

**John 3:16-21**

> <sup class="verse-num">16</sup> For this is how God loved the world: He gave his one and only Son, so that everyone who believes in him will not perish but have eternal life. <sup class="verse-num">17</sup> God sent his Son into the world not to judge the world, but to save the world through him. <sup class="verse-num">18</sup> There is no judgment against anyone who believes in him. But anyone who does not believe in him has already been judged for not believing in God's one and only Son. <sup class="verse-num">19</sup> And the judgment is based on this fact: God's light came into the world, but people loved the darkness more than the light, for their actions were evil. <sup class="verse-num">20</sup> All who do evil hate the light and refuse to go near it for fear their sins will be exposed. <sup class="verse-num">21</sup> But those who do what is right come to the light so others can see that they are doing what God wants.

## The Story

John's Gospel often uses the stark contrast between light and darkness to describe the human condition. In the ancient world, night was a time of genuine danger and vulnerability, offering a cover for things people wanted to keep hidden. When this text was written, the early church was figuring out what it meant to follow a teacher who claimed to be the very presence of God on earth. The writer frames the arrival of this teacher not as a gavel falling in a courtroom, but as a rescue mission. The divine purpose was entirely rooted in profound affection for a broken world. The tragedy highlighted here is that humanity often prefers the familiar safety of the shadows over the exposure of the dawn.

## Application for Today

We often carry a quiet fear that if the divine were to truly look at us, the result would be harsh judgment. Yet this passage insists that God's primary posture toward us is one of rescue, driven by an expansive and self-giving love. When we hide our flaws, our shame, or our missteps in the dark, we are only isolating ourselves from the very source of healing. Stepping into the light does not mean we are suddenly perfect, but rather that we are willing to be seen and loved exactly as we are. We are invited to drop our defenses and trust that the light exposes our wounds not to mock them, but to mend them. Healing begins the moment we allow ourselves to be fully known.
