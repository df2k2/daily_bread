## The Reading (NLT)

He has created us anew in Christ Jesus, so we can do the good things he planned for us long ago.

## The Story

The letter to the Ephesians addresses people living in a massive cultural and economic hub, surrounded by monuments to human achievement and imperial glory. In such a society, a person's worth was entirely tied to their status, their lineage, and what they could produce or conquer. The author flips this exhausting script by contrasting the hollow, death-dealing ways of the world with an entirely different spiritual economy. He describes the early believers not as cogs in a societal machine, but as people who have been pulled from a state of spiritual lifelessness into vibrant existence. This transformation has absolutely nothing to do with their own effort or societal climbing. Instead, their rescue is framed as a pure, unearned gift from a deeply merciful Creator. They are redefined not by what they achieve, but by the one who made them.

## Application for Today

We still live in a world that constantly asks us to prove our worth through our accomplishments, our resumes, and our hustle. It is incredibly easy to internalize the idea that we must earn our right to take up space, even in our relationship with the Divine. Yet this passage dismantles the exhausting need to justify our own existence. We are invited to step off the treadmill of performance and simply accept that we are deeply loved without having to work for it. Only from this place of profound rest can we begin to live out our true purpose. The good we do in the world is no longer a desperate attempt to win approval, but a natural, joyful response to the grace we have already received. We are invited to see ourselves as a carefully crafted work of art, designed to bring light to the spaces we inhabit.
