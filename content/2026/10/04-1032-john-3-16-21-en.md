## The Reading (NLT)

**John 3:16-21**

> <sup class="verse-num">16</sup> For this is how God loved the world: He gave his one and only Son, so that everyone who believes in him will not perish but have eternal life. <sup class="verse-num">17</sup> God sent his Son into the world not to judge the world, but to save the world through him. <sup class="verse-num">18</sup> There is no judgment against anyone who believes in him. But anyone who does not believe in him has already been judged for not believing in God's one and only Son. <sup class="verse-num">19</sup> And the judgment is based on this fact: God's light came into the world, but people loved the darkness more than the light, for their actions were evil. <sup class="verse-num">20</sup> All who do evil hate the light and refuse to go near it for fear their sins will be exposed. <sup class="verse-num">21</sup> But those who do what is right come to the light so others can see that they are doing what God wants.

## The Story

The author of this Gospel frames the arrival of Jesus not as a courtroom gavel coming down, but as a lantern being carried into a pitch-black room. In the ancient world, darkness offered a convenient cover for anything one wanted to hide, while light exposed every flaw and secret. The text describes a natural human instinct to retreat into the shadows when we feel ashamed or defensive. Yet, the divine intent introduced here flips the expectation of a harsh, punishing deity. Instead, the ultimate rescue mission is motivated entirely by profound affection for the entire cosmos.

## Application for Today

We often carry an assumption that stepping into God's presence will result in immediate condemnation for our failures. It is much easier to stay hidden, keeping our struggles and mistakes safely tucked away in the dark. But the invitation here is to realize that the light is not meant to interrogate us, but to heal us. When we stop hiding and step forward, we discover that the exposure we feared is actually the beginning of freedom. We are invited to let go of our secrecy and allow ourselves to be seen, fully trusting the motives of the one holding the lantern.
