## The Reading (NLT)

**Ephesians 2:1-10**

> <sup class="verse-num">1</sup> Once you were dead because of your disobedience and your many sins. <sup class="verse-num">2</sup> You used to live in sin, just like the rest of the world, obeying the devil the commander of the powers in the unseen world. He is the spirit at work in the hearts of those who refuse to obey God. <sup class="verse-num">3</sup> All of us used to live that way, following the passionate desires and inclinations of our sinful nature. By our very nature we were subject to God's anger, just like everyone else. <sup class="verse-num">4</sup> But God is so rich in mercy, and he loved us so much, <sup class="verse-num">5</sup> that even though we were dead because of our sins, he gave us life when he raised Christ from the dead. (It is only by God's grace that you have been saved!) <sup class="verse-num">6</sup> For he raised us from the dead along with Christ and seated us with him in the heavenly realms because we are united with Christ Jesus. <sup class="verse-num">7</sup> So God can point to us in all future ages as examples of the incredible wealth of his grace and kindness toward us, as shown in all he has done for us who are united with Christ Jesus. <sup class="verse-num">8</sup> God saved you by his grace when you believed. And you can't take credit for this; it is a gift from God. <sup class="verse-num">9</sup> Salvation is not a reward for the good things we have done, so none of us can boast about it. <sup class="verse-num">10</sup> For we are God's masterpiece. He has created us anew in Christ Jesus, so we can do the good things he planned for us long ago.

## The Story

In the ancient Greco-Roman world, status was often built on personal achievement, lineage, and public boasting. The letter to the Ephesians radically undercuts this cultural norm by describing humanity's natural spiritual state as entirely helpless and alienated from the divine. The author paints a stark contrast between a past defined by destructive impulses and a present defined by unearned rescue. This rescue hinges entirely on divine mercy rather than human effort, stripping away any basis for spiritual pride. The transformative pivot of the passage rests on God's abundant love stepping into the mess of human dysfunction. Believers are described not as self-made successes, but as rescued people who have been brought back to life.

## Application for Today

It is easy to slip into the exhausting mindset that we must earn our worth through constant effort and moral perfection. We often treat our spiritual lives like a resume we are building to impress the divine. Yet this passage reminds us that our transformation is entirely a gift, freeing us from the heavy burden of trying to save ourselves. When we grasp that we are already entirely loved and remade by grace, our perspective shifts. We are freed to look around and participate in the good things God is already doing in the world. Our acts of compassion and service are no longer desperate attempts to prove our value, but the natural expression of a life that has been beautifully restored.
