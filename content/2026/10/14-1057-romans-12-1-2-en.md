## The Reading (NLT)

**Romans 12:1-2**

> <sup class="verse-num">1</sup> And so, dear brothers and sisters, I plead with you to give your bodies to God because of all he has done for you. Let them be a living and holy sacrifice the kind he will find acceptable. This is truly the way to worship him. <sup class="verse-num">2</sup> Don't copy the behavior and customs of this world, but let God transform you into a new person by changing the way you think. Then you will learn to know God's will for you, which is good and pleasing and perfect.

## The Story

Paul's letter to the early believers in Rome marks a turning point here, shifting from deep theology to the practical rhythm of daily faith. In the ancient world, worship was universally understood as bringing a dead animal to an altar. The writer completely flips this cultural expectation, suggesting instead that a believer's own ongoing, breathing life is the actual offering. This redefines sacred duty not as a ritual performed in a temple, but as a continuous dedication of ordinary actions. The surrounding culture of the empire had its own powerful currents and expectations for how people should live, think, and interact. To resist being shaped by those imperial pressures required a fundamental rewiring of their internal perspective.

## Application for Today

We are constantly being molded by the culture around us, whether through the media we consume, the pace of our work, or the values of our society. It is incredibly easy to absorb these external pressures without even realizing we are conforming to them. The invitation here is to pause and allow divine grace to interrupt our default settings. When we intentionally open our minds to a different way of seeing the world, our daily habits begin to shift. We discover that true devotion is not confined to a Sunday morning service, but is lived out in how we treat others, handle our resources, and approach our struggles. As our perspective changes, we become better equipped to recognize the good and life-giving paths laid out before us.
