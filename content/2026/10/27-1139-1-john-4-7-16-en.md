## The Reading (NLT)

**1 John 4:7-16**

> <sup class="verse-num">7</sup> Dear friends, let us continue to love one another, for love comes from God. Anyone who loves is a child of God and knows God. <sup class="verse-num">8</sup> But anyone who does not love does not know God, for God is love. <sup class="verse-num">9</sup> God showed how much he loved us by sending his one and only Son into the world so that we might have eternal life through him. <sup class="verse-num">10</sup> This is real love not that we loved God, but that he loved us and sent his Son as a sacrifice to take away our sins. <sup class="verse-num">11</sup> Dear friends, since God loved us that much, we surely ought to love each other. <sup class="verse-num">12</sup> No one has ever seen God. But if we love each other, God lives in us, and his love is brought to full expression in us. <sup class="verse-num">13</sup> And God has given us his Spirit as proof that we live in him and he in us. <sup class="verse-num">14</sup> Furthermore, we have seen with our own eyes and now testify that the Father sent his Son to be the Savior of the world. <sup class="verse-num">15</sup> All who declare that Jesus is the Son of God have God living in them, and they live in God. <sup class="verse-num">16</sup> We know how much God loves us, and we have put our trust in his love. God is love, and all who live in love live in God, and God lives in them.

## The Story

The early Christian community receiving this letter was fracturing under the weight of internal divisions and competing claims about spiritual truth. Some members believed they possessed a superior, secret knowledge of the divine that elevated them above ordinary believers. The writer counters this by completely redefining what it means to know the Creator. True spiritual maturity, the letter insists, is not found in esoteric wisdom but in the tangible practice of caring for one another. The author anchors this argument in the very nature of the divine, explaining that the Creator’s essence was definitively revealed through the sacrifice of the Son. Therefore, any claim to spiritual enlightenment that bypasses human compassion is ultimately empty.

## Application for Today

We often search for the divine in spectacular moments, quiet mountaintops, or deep theological insights. Yet this passage offers a profoundly grounded alternative for experiencing the sacred in our daily lives. It suggests that the invisible Creator becomes tangibly present right in the middle of our ordinary interactions. When you choose patience with a frustrating coworker, offer forgiveness to a friend, or show up for a neighbor in need, you are doing more than just being kind. You are actually participating in the divine nature and making the unseen reality of heaven visible on earth. Our active care for one another is the very space where the sacred chooses to dwell.
