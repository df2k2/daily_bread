## The Reading (NLT)

**1 John 4:7-16**

> <sup class="verse-num">7</sup> Dear friends, let us continue to love one another, for love comes from God. Anyone who loves is a child of God and knows God. <sup class="verse-num">8</sup> But anyone who does not love does not know God, for God is love. <sup class="verse-num">9</sup> God showed how much he loved us by sending his one and only Son into the world so that we might have eternal life through him. <sup class="verse-num">10</sup> This is real love not that we loved God, but that he loved us and sent his Son as a sacrifice to take away our sins. <sup class="verse-num">11</sup> Dear friends, since God loved us that much, we surely ought to love each other. <sup class="verse-num">12</sup> No one has ever seen God. But if we love each other, God lives in us, and his love is brought to full expression in us. <sup class="verse-num">13</sup> And God has given us his Spirit as proof that we live in him and he in us. <sup class="verse-num">14</sup> Furthermore, we have seen with our own eyes and now testify that the Father sent his Son to be the Savior of the world. <sup class="verse-num">15</sup> All who declare that Jesus is the Son of God have God living in them, and they live in God. <sup class="verse-num">16</sup> We know how much God loves us, and we have put our trust in his love. God is love, and all who live in love live in God, and God lives in them.

## The Story

The early Christians lived in a world where deities were often viewed as capricious, distant, or demanding transactional sacrifices. Into this cultural landscape, the writer of this letter introduces a radical definition of the divine. He asserts that the Creator's fundamental essence is not just power or intellect, but self-giving affection. This wasn't merely an abstract philosophy. The author anchors this claim in a historical event, pointing to the arrival of the Son as the ultimate, tangible proof of the Creator's intentions toward humanity. For this early community, knowing the divine was no longer about esoteric secrets or appeasing an angry sky; it was about participating in a relationship initiated by a deeply invested Creator.

## Application for Today

We often treat love as a fleeting emotion or a reward for good behavior. Yet, the invitation here is to view love as the very atmosphere in which we are meant to live and move. When we struggle to feel connected to the divine, we are reminded that the invisible becomes visible in the ordinary act of caring for one another. We do not have to conjure this capacity from our own depleted reserves. Because the source of this affection already dwells within us, our task is simply to let it flow outward. Every time we choose patience, generosity, or forgiveness, we become living evidence of a God who refuses to remain unseen.
