## The Reading (NLT)

**Psalm 23**

> <sup class="verse-num">1</sup> The Lord is my shepherd; I have all that I need. <sup class="verse-num">2</sup> He lets me rest in green meadows; he leads me beside peaceful streams. <sup class="verse-num">3</sup> He renews my strength. He guides me along right paths, bringing honor to his name. <sup class="verse-num">4</sup> Even when I walk through the darkest valley, I will not be afraid, for you are close beside me. Your rod and your staff protect and comfort me. <sup class="verse-num">5</sup> You prepare a feast for me in the presence of my enemies. You honor me by anointing my head with oil. My cup overflows with blessings. <sup class="verse-num">6</sup> Surely your goodness and unfailing love will pursue me all the days of my life, and I will live in the house of the Lord forever.

## The Story

David’s famous song draws deeply on his own lived experience tending flocks in the rugged Judean wilderness. In that harsh landscape, finding safe pastures and still waters required intimate knowledge of the terrain and constant vigilance. The imagery shifts seamlessly from a protective guide in dangerous ravines to a gracious host offering lavish hospitality. In the ancient Near East, anointing a guest with oil and providing an overflowing cup were ultimate signs of honor and sanctuary. The poem moves from talking about the divine guide to speaking directly to him at the moment of deepest vulnerability. This shift highlights a profound intimacy that transforms fear into quiet confidence.

## Application for Today

We often spend our days running at a frantic pace, searching for security in our achievements or bank accounts. Yet this ancient song invites us to recognize a provision that does not depend on our own exhausting efforts. When we encounter periods of grief or uncertainty, the promise is not that the shadows will magically disappear. Instead, the promise is a steadfast presence walking alongside us. We are offered a table of grace right in the middle of our conflicts and anxieties. Recognizing this enduring companionship allows us to finally stop striving and simply rest.
