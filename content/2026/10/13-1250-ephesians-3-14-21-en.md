## The Reading (NLT)

**Ephesians 3:14-21**

> <sup class="verse-num">14</sup> When I think of all this, I fall to my knees and pray to the Father, <sup class="verse-num">15</sup> the Creator of everything in heaven and on earth. <sup class="verse-num">16</sup> I pray that from his glorious, unlimited resources he will empower you with inner strength through his Spirit. <sup class="verse-num">17</sup> Then Christ will make his home in your hearts as you trust in him. Your roots will grow down into God's love and keep you strong. <sup class="verse-num">18</sup> And may you have the power to understand, as all God's people should, how wide, how long, how high, and how deep his love is. <sup class="verse-num">19</sup> May you experience the love of Christ, though it is too great to understand fully. Then you will be made complete with all the fullness of life and power that comes from God. <sup class="verse-num">20</sup> Now all glory to God, who is able, through his mighty power at work within us, to accomplish infinitely more than we might ask or think. <sup class="verse-num">21</sup> Glory to him in the church and in Christ Jesus through all generations forever and ever! Amen.

## The Story

Paul is writing to a community trying to understand their place in a radically new kind of family. Rather than offering a theological lecture, he is moved to a posture of absolute reverence, bowing before the Creator who holds all people together. His prayer for them uses imagery that is both architectural and agricultural. He envisions their spiritual lives as trees needing deep roots and buildings requiring a solid foundation, both constructed entirely out of divine affection. He wants them to grasp dimensions of grace that are ultimately beyond human measurement. The goal is not intellectual mastery, but an experiential filling up with a presence that defies logic.

## Application for Today

It is easy to live with a small view of what we are worth or what we can expect from the divine. We often approach our spiritual lives hoping for just enough strength to get by, or assuming affection is conditional on our performance. Yet this passage invites us into a reality where compassion has no borders. We are asked to trust that the foundation beneath us is an affection so wide and deep it cannot be exhausted. When we feel depleted, we do not need to muster up our own energy. We are invited to simply sink our roots into a love that is already prepared to accomplish far more within us than our minds could ever conceive.
