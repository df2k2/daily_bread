## The Reading (NLT)

**Ephesians 3:14-21**

> <sup class="verse-num">14</sup> When I think of all this, I fall to my knees and pray to the Father, <sup class="verse-num">15</sup> the Creator of everything in heaven and on earth. <sup class="verse-num">16</sup> I pray that from his glorious, unlimited resources he will empower you with inner strength through his Spirit. <sup class="verse-num">17</sup> Then Christ will make his home in your hearts as you trust in him. Your roots will grow down into God's love and keep you strong. <sup class="verse-num">18</sup> And may you have the power to understand, as all God's people should, how wide, how long, how high, and how deep his love is. <sup class="verse-num">19</sup> May you experience the love of Christ, though it is too great to understand fully. Then you will be made complete with all the fullness of life and power that comes from God. <sup class="verse-num">20</sup> Now all glory to God, who is able, through his mighty power at work within us, to accomplish infinitely more than we might ask or think. <sup class="verse-num">21</sup> Glory to him in the church and in Christ Jesus through all generations forever and ever! Amen.

## The Story

The author of this letter is writing from a place of physical confinement, yet his imagination roams the cosmos. Ephesus was a bustling, diverse city where early believers often felt small and vulnerable against the backdrop of grand temples and imperial power. Instead of praying for their immediate rescue or political influence, the writer asks for something far more foundational. He points to the imagery of deep roots and vast architecture to describe the environment these believers are meant to inhabit. The prayer shifts the focus from external circumstances to an internal transformation grounded in an expansive, immeasurable affection. It is a bold vision for a community that might otherwise feel entirely marginalized.

## Application for Today

We often measure our lives by what we can manage, control, or logically comprehend. When faced with overwhelming challenges, our prayers tend to shrink to the size of our immediate anxieties. Yet this ancient petition invites us to step into a reality that defies our limited geometry. We are called to sink our roots into a love that extends further than our deepest fears and reaches higher than our loftiest ambitions. Trusting this process means accepting that we are being filled by a presence that outpaces our own imagination. We do not have to figure everything out; we only need to remain planted in the soil of that enduring grace.
