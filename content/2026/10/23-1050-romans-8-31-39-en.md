## The Reading (NLT)

**Romans 8:31-39**

> <sup class="verse-num">31</sup> What shall we say about such wonderful things as these? If God is for us, who can ever be against us? <sup class="verse-num">32</sup> Since he did not spare even his own Son but gave him up for us all, won't he also give us everything else? <sup class="verse-num">33</sup> Who dares accuse us whom God has chosen for his own? No one for God himself has given us right standing with himself. <sup class="verse-num">34</sup> Who then will condemn us? No one for Christ Jesus died for us and was raised to life for us, and he is sitting in the place of honor at God's right hand, pleading for us. <sup class="verse-num">35</sup> Can anything ever separate us from Christ's love? Does it mean he no longer loves us if we have trouble or calamity, or are persecuted, or hungry, or destitute, or in danger, or threatened with death? <sup class="verse-num">36</sup> (As the Scriptures say, For your sake we are killed every day; we are being slaughtered like sheep.) <sup class="verse-num">37</sup> No, despite all these things, overwhelming victory is ours through Christ, who loved us. <sup class="verse-num">38</sup> And I am convinced that nothing can ever separate us from God's love. Neither death nor life, neither angels nor demons, neither our fears for today nor our worries about tomorrow not even the powers of hell can separate us from God's love. <sup class="verse-num">39</sup> No power in the sky above or in the earth below indeed, nothing in all creation will ever be able to separate us from the love of God that is revealed in Christ Jesus our Lord.

## The Story

Paul wrote to a community living in the shadow of an empire that demanded absolute allegiance. The early Roman Christians faced immense social pressure, physical danger, and the constant threat of violence. In his letter, he addresses their deepest vulnerabilities, acknowledging the brutal realities of hunger, destitution, and persecution. He uses the language of a courtroom, where charges might be brought against them, only to be dismissed by the highest judge. Yet, his argument shifts from legal defense to cosmic assurance. He surveys the entire universe, from the unseen spiritual realm to the physical world, concluding that no force exists that can sever their connection to the divine.

## Application for Today

We often look at our own hardships and wonder if we have somehow been abandoned. When grief, anxiety, or material struggles overwhelm us, it is easy to feel isolated from any source of grace. This passage invites us to radically shift our perspective on suffering. Hardships are not a sign of divine distance, nor do our failures give anyone the right to condemn us. Instead, we are held fast by a love that has already bridged the greatest possible gaps. Whatever you are facing today, whether it is a quiet internal fear or a very public struggle, you are securely anchored. Nothing you encounter can untie the knot that holds you.
