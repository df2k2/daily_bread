## The Reading (NLT)

**John 15:1-11**

> <sup class="verse-num">1</sup> I am the true grapevine, and my Father is the gardener. <sup class="verse-num">2</sup> He cuts off every branch of mine that doesn't produce fruit, and he prunes the branches that do bear fruit so they will produce even more. <sup class="verse-num">3</sup> You have already been pruned and purified by the message I have given you. <sup class="verse-num">4</sup> Remain in me, and I will remain in you. For a branch cannot produce fruit if it is severed from the vine, and you cannot be fruitful unless you remain in me. <sup class="verse-num">5</sup> Yes, I am the vine; you are the branches. Those who remain in me, and I in them, will produce much fruit. For apart from me you can do nothing. <sup class="verse-num">6</sup> Anyone who does not remain in me is thrown away like a useless branch and withers. Such branches are gathered into a pile to be burned. <sup class="verse-num">7</sup> But if you remain in me and my words remain in you, you may ask for anything you want, and it will be granted! <sup class="verse-num">8</sup> When you produce much fruit, you are my true disciples. This brings great glory to my Father. <sup class="verse-num">9</sup> I have loved you even as the Father has loved me. Remain in my love. <sup class="verse-num">10</sup> When you obey my commandments, you remain in my love, just as I obey my Father's commandments and remain in his love. <sup class="verse-num">11</sup> I have told you these things so that you will be filled with my joy. Yes, your joy will overflow!

## The Story

In the ancient agrarian world, vineyards were a familiar sight, demanding constant and careful attention. Grapevines require aggressive maintenance to thrive, as untended branches quickly grow wild and drain the plant's energy without yielding anything useful. A skilled viticulturist knows exactly where to cut, removing what is dead and trimming back what is healthy so it can flourish even further. This agricultural reality serves as a powerful metaphor for spiritual vitality. The focus here is not on the independent strength of a single offshoot, but on its vital connection to the central root system. Life flows from the core outward, making the branch's sole responsibility to stay attached.

## Application for Today

We often measure our spiritual success by how much we can accomplish on our own, treating faith as a checklist of good deeds. Yet this passage shifts our attention away from frantic production and toward quiet connection. The pruning process can feel painful or disorienting, as things we thought were essential are stripped away. However, this removal is not an act of punishment, but of tender cultivation by a gardener who wants us to thrive. True spiritual vitality comes from resting in a love that already holds us secure. When we stop striving and simply focus on remaining deeply rooted in that love, the growth happens naturally. The ultimate result is not just a life of purpose, but a profound, overflowing joy.
