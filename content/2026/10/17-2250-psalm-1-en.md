## The Reading (NLT)

**Psalm 1**

> <sup class="verse-num">1</sup> Oh, the joys of those who do not follow the advice of the wicked, or stand around with sinners, or join in with mockers. <sup class="verse-num">2</sup> But they delight in the law of the Lord, meditating on it day and night. <sup class="verse-num">3</sup> They are like trees planted along the riverbank, bearing fruit each season. Their leaves never wither, and they prosper in all they do. <sup class="verse-num">4</sup> But not the wicked! They are like worthless chaff, scattered by the wind. <sup class="verse-num">5</sup> They will be condemned at the time of judgment. Sinners will have no place among the godly. <sup class="verse-num">6</sup> For the Lord watches over the path of the godly, but the path of the wicked leads to destruction.

## The Story

As the gateway to the ancient collection of Hebrew songs and prayers, this opening text presents a stark contrast between two ways of living. The writer relies on agricultural imagery familiar to the ancient Near East, describing a life anchored near a steady water source. Such a life is not immune to changing seasons or harsh weather, but its secret lies beneath the surface. Because it is constantly nourished, it maintains its vitality and produces what is needed at exactly the right time. In contrast, the alternative path is described as weightless and easily blown away, lacking any anchor when the winds rise. The text invites the reader to consider where they are drawing their foundational sustenance.

## Application for Today

We are constantly offered advice on how to live, often from voices that encourage cynicism or celebrate fleeting success. Yet this ancient wisdom suggests that true resilience comes from a quieter, more deliberate focus. When we intentionally anchor our attention in divine wisdom, we develop an inner stability that sustains us through periods of drought or exhaustion. We do not have to force our own growth or manufacture our own strength. Instead, by simply remaining close to the source of life, we are slowly transformed. Our capacity to endure and offer something good to the world becomes a natural byproduct of where we have chosen to plant ourselves.
