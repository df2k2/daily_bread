## The Reading (NLT)

**Hebrews 12:1-3**

> <sup class="verse-num">1</sup> Therefore, since we are surrounded by such a huge crowd of witnesses to the life of faith, let us strip off every weight that slows us down, especially the sin that so easily trips us up. And let us run with endurance the race God has set before us. <sup class="verse-num">2</sup> We do this by keeping our eyes on Jesus, the champion who initiates and perfects our faith. Because of the joy awaiting him, he endured the cross, disregarding its shame. Now he is seated in the place of honor beside God's throne. <sup class="verse-num">3</sup> Think of all the hostility he endured from sinful people; then you won't become weary and give up.

## The Story

The writer of Hebrews has just spent an entire chapter listing heroes of the past, highlighting people who trusted God through immense struggles. Now, the author shifts to a vivid athletic metaphor to encourage an audience facing exhaustion and the temptation to quit. The imagery evokes a grand stadium where past heroes watch from the stands, cheering on the current runners. To run effectively, an ancient athlete would shed heavy robes and any other distractions. The ultimate example of this focus is Jesus. He pushed through the agony and humiliation of his execution by keeping his mind on the profound joy waiting on the other side.

## Application for Today

Life often feels less like a quick sprint and more like an exhausting marathon over rugged terrain. Along the way, we accumulate emotional weight, carrying anxieties, regrets, and habits that tangle our feet and make every step harder. The invitation here is to intentionally drop that heavy baggage. When we feel completely drained and ready to quit, we are encouraged to look ahead, just as a runner locks their eyes on the finish line. Focusing on Christ's endurance reorients our perspective. We realize we aren't running alone, and we can find the strength to take just one more step.
