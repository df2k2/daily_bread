## The Reading (NLT)

**1 Corinthians 13**

> <sup class="verse-num">1</sup> If I could speak all the languages of earth and of angels, but didn't love others, I would only be a noisy gong or a clanging cymbal. <sup class="verse-num">2</sup> If I had the gift of prophecy, and if I understood all of God's secret plans and possessed all knowledge, and if I had such faith that I could move mountains, but didn't love others, I would be nothing. <sup class="verse-num">3</sup> If I gave everything I have to the poor and even sacrificed my body, I could boast about it; but if I didn't love others, I would have gained nothing. <sup class="verse-num">4</sup> Love is patient and kind. All that I know now is partial and incomplete, but then I will know everything completely, just as God now knows me completely. <sup class="verse-num">13</sup> Three things will last forever faith, hope, and love and the greatest of these is love.

## The Story

Paul was writing to a community obsessed with status and spectacular spiritual displays. The church in Corinth prized knowledge, eloquence, and dramatic gifts, often using them to compete with one another. In response to their rivalry, the apostle introduces a radically different metric for a meaningful life. He explains that even the most impressive acts of faith or generosity are entirely empty if they are not driven by genuine care for others. He shifts their focus from temporary human achievements to the eternal nature of divine affection. To Paul, spiritual maturity isn't about knowing everything; it is about outgrowing self-centeredness and learning to care deeply for the people around us.

## Application for Today

We still live in a world that applauds talent, intellect, and grand gestures. It is easy to measure our worth by what we accomplish, what we know, or how much we sacrifice. Yet this passage invites us to evaluate our lives through a much quieter, more demanding lens. True maturity is found in our daily patience, our refusal to keep score, and our willingness to endure difficult seasons alongside others. Our current understanding of the world is limited, and our achievements will eventually fade into the background. What remains, and what truly shapes eternity, is the quiet, enduring practice of loving one another.
