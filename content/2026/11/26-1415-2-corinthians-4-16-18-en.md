## The Reading (NLT)

**2 Corinthians 4:16-18**

> <sup class="verse-num">16</sup> That is why we never give up. Though our bodies are dying, our spirits are being renewed every day. <sup class="verse-num">17</sup> For our present troubles are small and won't last very long. Yet they produce for us a glory that vastly outweighs them and will last forever! <sup class="verse-num">18</sup> So we don't look at the troubles we can see now; rather, we fix our gaze on things that cannot be seen. For the things we see now will soon be gone, but the things we cannot see will last forever.

## The Story

Paul’s letters to the church in Corinth are deeply personal, often exposing the raw edges of his own ministry. By this point in his life, he had endured shipwrecks, beatings, imprisonment, and chronic illness. His body was literally wearing out from the demands of his calling. Yet, he writes to a community facing their own intense pressures, reframing their collective exhaustion. In the ancient Greco-Roman worldview, physical suffering was often seen as a sign of divine disfavor, but the author completely subverts this by suggesting that outward decay is the canvas for inward renewal.

## Application for Today

It is incredibly easy to be consumed by the immediate pressures right in front of us. Chronic pain, financial stress, and emotional fatigue demand our full attention, often leaving us feeling depleted. But this text invites us to expand our field of vision beyond our temporary exhaustion. The spiritual renewal happening inside us doesn't negate our physical or mental weariness, but it does anchor us in something deeper. When we feel ourselves wearing down, we can trust that an unseen, enduring strength is quietly being built within us. We are invited to look past the immediate heavy burdens and rest in the reality of a love that outlasts them all.
