## The Reading (NLT)

**Ephesians 3:14-21**

> <sup class="verse-num">14</sup> When I think of all this, I fall to my knees and pray to the Father, <sup class="verse-num">15</sup> the Creator of everything in heaven and on earth. <sup class="verse-num">16</sup> I pray that from his glorious, unlimited resources he will empower you with inner strength through his Spirit. <sup class="verse-num">17</sup> Then Christ will make his home in your hearts as you trust in him. Your roots will grow down into God's love and keep you strong. <sup class="verse-num">18</sup> And may you have the power to understand, as all God's people should, how wide, how long, how high, and how deep his love is. <sup class="verse-num">19</sup> May you experience the love of Christ, though it is too great to understand fully. Then you will be made complete with all the fullness of life and power that comes from God. <sup class="verse-num">20</sup> Now all glory to God, who is able, through his mighty power at work within us, to accomplish infinitely more than we might ask or think. <sup class="verse-num">21</sup> Glory to him in the church and in Christ Jesus through all generations forever and ever! Amen.

## The Story

The apostle writing to the early communities in Ephesus shifts from theological instruction to a posture of deep reverence. He bows before the Creator of all things, recognizing that spiritual vitality requires more than just intellectual understanding. His prayer focuses on the inner architecture of the human heart, asking that it be fortified by divine strength. He uses agricultural metaphors, envisioning believers as deeply rooted plants drawing nourishment from a secure foundation. He describes a multidimensional affection that defies simple measurement, stretching in every possible direction. Acknowledging that this reality bypasses human logic, he invites the community into a profound mystery rather than a textbook definition.

## Application for Today

We often try to contain the divine within our own limited expectations and categories. Yet this ancient prayer invites us to experience a reality that is far wider and deeper than our current understanding. When we feel spiritually dry or overwhelmed, our first impulse might be to strive harder or search for more information. Instead, we are encouraged to simply let our roots sink further into a compassion that already surrounds us. Trusting in this expansive affection transforms our inner lives, making room for a peace that logic cannot produce. We are reminded that the quiet work happening within us is not constrained by our own limited imagination. We can rest in the assurance that grace operates on a scale far beyond our everyday requests.
