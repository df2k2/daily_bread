## The Reading (NLT)

**1 Peter 5:6-11**

> <sup class="verse-num">6</sup> So humble yourselves under the mighty power of God, and at the right time he will lift you up in honor. <sup class="verse-num">7</sup> Give all your worries and cares to God, for he cares about you. <sup class="verse-num">8</sup> Stay alert! Remember that your family of believers all over the world is going through the same kind of suffering you are. <sup class="verse-num">10</sup> In his kindness God called you to share in his eternal glory by means of Christ Jesus. So after you have suffered a little while, he will restore, support, and strengthen you, and he will place you on a firm foundation. <sup class="verse-num">11</sup> All power to him forever! Amen.

## The Story

The early Christian communities were scattered and facing intense social pressure, marginalization, and active hostility. The writer of this letter understands that this kind of sustained pressure breeds deep anxiety and a sense of isolation. To counter this, he reminds them that their struggles are not unique to their local congregation, but are shared by believers across the known world. In the face of an adversary that uses fear and exhaustion to break their spirit, the readers are urged to remain vigilant but not to carry the burden alone. The profound advice given here is to release their heavy, accumulated worries directly into the care of a God who is intimately attentive to their well-being.

## Application for Today

When we face prolonged difficulties, our natural instinct is often to grip our problems tighter and try to manage every outcome. We become exhausted by the sheer weight of our own anxieties. Yet, true spiritual resilience begins with the humility to admit we cannot hold it all together. By intentionally handing our fears over to God, we free ourselves to stand firm in the present moment. We are reminded that our current struggles, though painful, are temporary and shared by a wider community of faith. Ultimately, it is not our own white-knuckled grip that sustains us, but the steady, restoring hand of God that places us on solid ground.
