## The Reading (NLT)

**Ephesians 3:14-21**

> <sup class="verse-num">14</sup> When I think of all this, I fall to my knees and pray to the Father, <sup class="verse-num">15</sup> the Creator of everything in heaven and on earth. <sup class="verse-num">16</sup> I pray that from his glorious, unlimited resources he will empower you with inner strength through his Spirit. <sup class="verse-num">17</sup> Then Christ will make his home in your hearts as you trust in him. Your roots will grow down into God's love and keep you strong. <sup class="verse-num">18</sup> And may you have the power to understand, as all God's people should, how wide, how long, how high, and how deep his love is. <sup class="verse-num">19</sup> May you experience the love of Christ, though it is too great to understand fully. Then you will be made complete with all the fullness of life and power that comes from God. <sup class="verse-num">20</sup> Now all glory to God, who is able, through his mighty power at work within us, to accomplish infinitely more than we might ask or think. <sup class="verse-num">21</sup> Glory to him in the church and in Christ Jesus through all generations forever and ever! Amen.

## The Story

Paul writes to a community living in a culturally and spiritually diverse city, where power and status often dictated human worth. He adopts a posture of profound humility, bowing down to address the Creator of all things. Rather than asking for a change in their external circumstances, his prayer focuses entirely on their internal reality. He envisions a spiritual architecture where love is both the foundation and the soil. He wants these early believers to grasp a paradox, recognizing that they are called to comprehend a love that ultimately surpasses human understanding. This grand vision culminates in a sweeping declaration of divine capability that stretches far beyond human imagination.

## Application for Today

We often approach our spiritual lives with a list of practical requests, seeking immediate solutions to our daily struggles. Yet this passage invites us to shift our focus inward, asking first for a deep, anchoring awareness of divine affection. When we feel overwhelmed by the demands of modern life, we can remember that our ultimate stability comes from being rooted in something far larger than ourselves. The vastness of this love is not meant to make us feel small, but rather to expand our capacity to live fully. We do not have to figure everything out or carry the weight of the world alone. There is an active, quiet power at work within us, capable of sustaining us in ways we cannot even begin to anticipate.
