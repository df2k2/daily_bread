## The Reading (NLT)

**John 14:1-7**

> <sup class="verse-num">1</sup> Don't let your hearts be troubled. Trust in God, and trust also in me. <sup class="verse-num">2</sup> There is more than enough room in my Father's home. If this were not so, would I have told you that I am going to prepare a place for you? <sup class="verse-num">3</sup> When everything is ready, I will come and get you, so that you will always be with me where I am. <sup class="verse-num">4</sup> And you know the way to where I am going. <sup class="verse-num">5</sup> No, we don't know, Lord, Thomas said. We have no idea where you are going, so how can we know the way? <sup class="verse-num">6</sup> Jesus told him, I am the way, the truth, and the life. No one can come to the Father except through me. <sup class="verse-num">7</sup> If you had really known me, you would know who my Father is. From now on, you do know him and have seen him!

## The Story

The context of this conversation is a final meal shared among friends, heavy with impending loss. The disciples are facing the collapse of their expectations as their teacher speaks of betrayal and departure. In a room thick with anxiety, they desperately want a concrete plan or a geographical destination to calm their fears. Thomas voices the collective panic, asking for clear directions to wherever this unknown journey leads. Instead of providing a schematic or a set of coordinates, the response they receive centers entirely on relationship. The destination and the route are bound up in the person standing right in front of them.

## Application for Today

When our own lives feel uncertain, we often look for a detailed blueprint to guarantee we are heading in the right direction. We want step-by-step instructions to navigate grief, transition, or spiritual doubt. Yet this passage suggests that navigating the unknown is less about having all the answers and more about trusting a companion. The divine response to human anxiety is not a detailed itinerary, but an invitation to look closely at the life of Christ. By observing how he moved through the world with compassion and truth, we discover the path we are meant to walk. The way forward is found not in a place, but in a presence.
