## The Reading (NLT)

**Isaiah 40:28-31**

> <sup class="verse-num">28</sup> Have you never heard? Have you never understood? The Lord is the everlasting God, the Creator of all the earth. He never grows weak or weary. No one can measure the depths of his understanding. <sup class="verse-num">29</sup> He gives power to the weak and strength to the powerless. <sup class="verse-num">30</sup> Even youths will become weak and tired, and young men will fall in exhaustion. <sup class="verse-num">31</sup> But those who trust in the Lord will find new strength. They will soar high on wings like eagles. They will run and not grow weary. They will walk and not faint.

## The Story

The prophet speaks to a community in exile, a people who feel forgotten and entirely drained of hope. They are looking at their own depleted resources and concluding that their future is over. In response, the text points them away from their own fragile endurance and toward the cosmic, unending energy of the Creator. The imagery acknowledges a universal truth that even the most vibrant and youthful among us will eventually hit a wall of exhaustion. Yet, it introduces a radical alternative to self-reliance, offering a profound exchange of human weakness for divine vitality.

## Application for Today

We live in a culture that prizes endless productivity and personal resilience, often pushing us until we crash. When we reach the end of our own strength, we tend to feel a sense of failure, as if we should have been able to keep going indefinitely. This passage invites us to accept our limits as a natural part of being human, not a flaw to be hidden. By shifting our trust from our own stamina to the Creator's limitless care, we open ourselves to a different kind of endurance. Sometimes this divine strength looks like soaring, but more often, it simply gives us just enough grace to keep walking forward without giving up.
