## The Reading (NLT)

**Psalm 1**

> <sup class="verse-num">1</sup> Oh, the joys of those who do not follow the advice of the wicked, or stand around with sinners, or join in with mockers. <sup class="verse-num">2</sup> But they delight in the law of the Lord, meditating on it day and night. <sup class="verse-num">3</sup> They are like trees planted along the riverbank, bearing fruit each season. Their leaves never wither, and they prosper in all they do. <sup class="verse-num">4</sup> But not the wicked! They are like worthless chaff, scattered by the wind. <sup class="verse-num">5</sup> They will be condemned at the time of judgment. Sinners will have no place among the godly. <sup class="verse-num">6</sup> For the Lord watches over the path of the godly, but the path of the wicked leads to destruction.

## The Story

The book of Psalms opens not with a prayer or a hymn, but with a piece of wisdom literature that sets the tone for the entire collection. It presents a stark contrast between two ways of living, using agricultural imagery that would be deeply familiar to an ancient agrarian society. The author describes a life anchored in divine instruction, comparing it to a tree intentionally cultivated near a constant water source. This isn't a wild sapling fighting for survival in the desert, but a nurtured plant guaranteed to receive the hydration it needs. In contrast, the alternative path is likened to the unusable husks of grain, completely at the mercy of shifting breezes. The passage frames human flourishing as a direct result of where we choose to draw our nourishment.

## Application for Today

We are constantly absorbing messages about who we should be and what we should value. This opening psalm invites us to examine the sources feeding our minds and hearts. When we anchor ourselves in spiritual practices and divine wisdom, we develop a resilience that sustains us through difficult seasons. This doesn't mean we will never face droughts, but rather that our roots will tap into a deeper well of peace when the surface gets dry. We can choose to be easily swayed by the fleeting opinions of the crowd, or we can sink our roots into something eternal. Cultivating a quiet, steady rhythm of reflection allows us to grow strong and bear fruit exactly when we are supposed to.
