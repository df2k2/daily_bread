## The Reading (NLT)

**Ephesians 3:14-21**

> <sup class="verse-num">14</sup> When I think of all this, I fall to my knees and pray to the Father, <sup class="verse-num">15</sup> the Creator of everything in heaven and on earth. <sup class="verse-num">16</sup> I pray that from his glorious, unlimited resources he will empower you with inner strength through his Spirit. <sup class="verse-num">17</sup> Then Christ will make his home in your hearts as you trust in him. Your roots will grow down into God's love and keep you strong. <sup class="verse-num">18</sup> And may you have the power to understand, as all God's people should, how wide, how long, how high, and how deep his love is. <sup class="verse-num">19</sup> May you experience the love of Christ, though it is too great to understand fully. Then you will be made complete with all the fullness of life and power that comes from God. <sup class="verse-num">20</sup> Now all glory to God, who is able, through his mighty power at work within us, to accomplish infinitely more than we might ask or think. <sup class="verse-num">21</sup> Glory to him in the church and in Christ Jesus through all generations forever and ever! Amen.

## The Story

The letter to the Ephesians was likely circulated among early Christian communities navigating a complex and multicultural world. The author pauses his theological instruction to offer a deeply personal prayer for his readers. He uses the imagery of a tree sinking its roots deep into the soil alongside the metaphor of a building resting on a solid foundation. Both images point to a stability not found in rigid rules but in a profound and expansive affection. The prayer asks that these early believers might somehow grasp the dimensions of a love that ultimately defies measurement. It is an invitation to experience a reality that stretches beyond human logic and fills the inner life with quiet power.

## Application for Today

We often try to measure our worth by what we can accomplish, earn, or understand. Yet this ancient prayer suggests our truest strength comes from simply being anchored in an affection we cannot fully comprehend. When we feel depleted or unsteady, the invitation is not to try harder, but to let our roots reach further down into the mystery of grace. We are held by a love that stretches wider than our mistakes and deeper than our deepest anxieties. Trusting this reality transforms how we live, shifting our focus from our own limitations to the boundless possibilities at work within us. We do not have to understand it all to be completely sustained by it.
