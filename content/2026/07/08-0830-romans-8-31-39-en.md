## The Reading (NLT)

**Romans 8:31-39**

> <sup class="verse-num">31</sup> What shall we say about such wonderful things as these? If God is for us, who can ever be against us? <sup class="verse-num">32</sup> Since he did not spare even his own Son but gave him up for us all, won't he also give us everything else? <sup class="verse-num">33</sup> Who dares accuse us whom God has chosen for his own? No one for God himself has given us right standing with himself. <sup class="verse-num">34</sup> Who then will condemn us? No one for Christ Jesus died for us and was raised to life for us, and he is sitting in the place of honor at God's right hand, pleading for us. <sup class="verse-num">35</sup> Can anything ever separate us from Christ's love? Neither death nor life, neither angels nor demons, neither our fears for today nor our worries about tomorrow not even the powers of hell can separate us from God's love. <sup class="verse-num">39</sup> No power in the sky above or in the earth below indeed, nothing in all creation will ever be able to separate us from the love of God that is revealed in Christ Jesus our Lord.

## The Story

Paul reaches the emotional and theological peak of his letter to the church in Rome. He has just spent chapters detailing human struggle, the weight of the law, and the groaning of creation. Now, he shifts into a courtroom metaphor, asking who could possibly bring a charge against those the Creator has already cleared. He lists the most terrifying realities of the ancient world, including famine, political execution, spiritual warfare, and the sheer unpredictability of the future. By naming these deep fears aloud, he validates the real suffering of his readers. Yet, he insists that these hardships are never evidence of divine abandonment.

## Application for Today

It is easy to look at the wreckage of a hard season and wonder if we have been forgotten. When relationships fracture or anxieties mount, our quiet fear is that we have somehow slipped out of divine grace. But this passage dismantles the idea that our circumstances dictate our standing. The devotion described here is not a fragile sentiment that shatters under pressure; it is a fierce, protective reality that has already crossed the threshold of death. Whatever you are carrying today, whether it is a lingering regret or a profound uncertainty about tomorrow, it does not have the power to sever you from love. You are held, completely and permanently.
