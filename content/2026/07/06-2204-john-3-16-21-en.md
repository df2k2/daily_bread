## The Reading (NLT)

**John 3:16-21**

> <sup class="verse-num">16</sup> For this is how God loved the world: He gave his one and only Son, so that everyone who believes in him will not perish but have eternal life. <sup class="verse-num">17</sup> God sent his Son into the world not to judge the world, but to save the world through him. <sup class="verse-num">18</sup> There is no judgment against anyone who believes in him. But anyone who does not believe in him has already been judged for not believing in God's one and only Son. <sup class="verse-num">19</sup> And the judgment is based on this fact: God's light came into the world, but people loved the darkness more than the light, for their actions were evil. <sup class="verse-num">20</sup> All who do evil hate the light and refuse to go near it for fear their sins will be exposed. <sup class="verse-num">21</sup> But those who do what is right come to the light so others can see that they are doing what God wants.

## The Story

The Gospel of John often uses stark contrasts to explain spiritual realities, framing the world in terms of light and darkness. In the ancient world, night was a time of genuine vulnerability and hiddenness, making light a powerful symbol for truth, safety, and divine presence. Here, the writer explains the core motivation behind the arrival of the Messiah. It was not a mission of hostility or punishment, though many expected a conquering judge to arrive and set the world straight by force. Instead, the narrative reveals an act of rescue driven entirely by love. The arrival of this divine light forces a choice to remain hidden in the shadows of shame or to step forward and be seen.

## Application for Today

It is deeply human to want to hide our flaws, our mistakes, and the parts of ourselves we fear are unacceptable. We often expect that being truly seen by the divine will result in immediate condemnation. Yet this passage dismantles that fear, assuring us that the ultimate purpose of God reaching out is rescue, not ruin. The light is not meant to be a harsh spotlight of interrogation, but rather a beacon leading us out of isolation. Stepping into that light requires vulnerability, asking us to trust that we will be met with grace rather than judgment. When we stop hiding, we allow the healing work of love to begin, discovering that the safest place to be is fully known.
