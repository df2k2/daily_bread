## The Reading (NLT)

**Psalm 1**

> <sup class="verse-num">1</sup> Oh, the joys of those who do not follow the advice of the wicked, or stand around with sinners, or join in with mockers. <sup class="verse-num">2</sup> But they delight in the law of the Lord, meditating on it day and night. <sup class="verse-num">3</sup> They are like trees planted along the riverbank, bearing fruit each season. Their leaves never wither, and they prosper in all they do. <sup class="verse-num">4</sup> But not the wicked! They are like worthless chaff, scattered by the wind. <sup class="verse-num">5</sup> They will be condemned at the time of judgment. Sinners will have no place among the godly. <sup class="verse-num">6</sup> For the Lord watches over the path of the godly, but the path of the wicked leads to destruction.

## The Story

The Book of Psalms opens not with a prayer, but with a vivid picture of two contrasting lives. Ancient readers lived in an arid climate where a thriving tree was a rarity, completely dependent on a constant water source. The psalmist uses this imagery to describe a life anchored in divine wisdom. Instead of being swayed by the cynical or destructive voices of the crowd, this person finds their joy in the rhythm of God's instruction. The alternative is depicted as chaff, the weightless husk of grain that is easily blown away by the slightest breeze. This opening poem sets the stage for the entire collection, suggesting that true stability comes from what we choose to absorb.

## Application for Today

We are constantly invited to draw our energy from the fast-paced, often cynical currents of modern culture. Yet this ancient song suggests a different way to thrive, one that relies on deep, hidden nourishment. When we intentionally anchor our minds in God's wisdom, we develop a resilience that outlasts passing storms. This kind of spiritual root system does not promise a life free of drought, but it ensures we will not wither when the heat comes. Growth happens quietly, and fruit arrives in its proper season. By choosing where we plant ourselves, we find the quiet strength to stand firm no matter which way the wind is blowing.
