## The Reading (NLT)

**Hebrews 12:1-3**

> <sup class="verse-num">1</sup> Therefore, since we are surrounded by such a huge crowd of witnesses to the life of faith, let us strip off every weight that slows us down, especially the sin that so easily trips us up. And let us run with endurance the race God has set before us. <sup class="verse-num">2</sup> We do this by keeping our eyes on Jesus, the champion who initiates and perfects our faith. Because of the joy awaiting him, he endured the cross, disregarding its shame. Now he is seated in the place of honor beside God's throne. <sup class="verse-num">3</sup> Think of all the hostility he endured from sinful people; then you won't become weary and give up.

## The Story

The original audience was exhausted. They had been facing social rejection and persecution for their faith, and many were tempted to just quit and go back to their old ways of life. The writer uses the imagery of a massive athletic competition in a grand stadium. In ancient races, runners would strip off all excess clothing and carry absolutely nothing that could slow them down. The author points them to the ultimate runner who already finished the hardest course imaginable. He faced unimaginable opposition and shame, but he pushed through the pain by focusing on the incredible joy waiting at the finish line.

## Application for Today

Life often feels less like a sprint and more like a grueling, uphill trek. Along the way, we tend to pick up extra baggage like resentment, anxiety, bad habits, and distractions that make every step heavier. When the road gets steep and our energy fades, we are invited to drop those burdens on the side of the trail. We do not have to carry them. Instead, we can fix our gaze on the one who already walked the most difficult path. By remembering how he endured the darkest valleys, we find the strength to take just one more step, and then another, without giving up.
