## The Reading (NLT)

**1 Corinthians 13**

> <sup class="verse-num">1</sup> If I could speak all the languages of earth and of angels, but didn't love others, I would only be a noisy gong or a clanging cymbal. <sup class="verse-num">2</sup> If I had the gift of prophecy, and if I understood all of God's secret plans and possessed all knowledge, and if I had such faith that I could move mountains, but didn't love others, I would be nothing. <sup class="verse-num">3</sup> If I gave everything I have to the poor and even sacrificed my body, I could boast about it; but if I didn't love others, I would have gained nothing. <sup class="verse-num">4</sup> Love is patient and kind. Love is not jealous or boastful or proud <sup class="verse-num">5</sup> or rude. It does not demand its own way. It is not irritable, and it keeps no record of being wronged. <sup class="verse-num">6</sup> It does not rejoice about injustice but rejoices whenever the truth wins out. <sup class="verse-num">7</sup> Love never gives up, never loses faith, is always hopeful, and endures through every circumstance. <sup class="verse-num">8</sup> Prophecy and speaking in unknown languages and special knowledge will become useless. But love will last forever! <sup class="verse-num">9</sup> Now our knowledge is partial and incomplete, and even the gift of prophecy reveals only part of the whole picture! <sup class="verse-num">10</sup> But when the time of perfection comes, these partial things will become useless. <sup class="verse-num">11</sup> When I was a child, I spoke and thought and reasoned as a child. All that I know now is partial and incomplete, but then I will know everything completely, just as God now knows me completely. <sup class="verse-num">13</sup> Three things will last forever faith, hope, and love and the greatest of these is love.

## The Story

The early Christian community in Corinth was fracturing over status. They were competing over who had the most impressive spiritual abilities, the deepest theological knowledge, and the most dramatic displays of devotion. Paul writes this letter to disrupt their obsession with spiritual performance. He points out that spectacular religious achievements are entirely hollow if they lack genuine care for others. In doing so, he shifts the focus from what people can do to who they are becoming. He frames spiritual maturity not as accumulating profound mysteries, but as practicing patient, unselfish connection.

## Application for Today

We often measure our lives by what we accomplish, what we know, or how much we sacrifice. Yet this passage strips away our impressive resumes and asks a much simpler, harder question about how we treat the people around us. True maturity is found in the quiet, daily work of setting aside our ego to make room for someone else. It is the choice to release grudges, to celebrate the truth, and to endure difficult seasons with grace. Everything else we build is temporary. What lasts, and what ultimately matters, is the love we have given away.
