## The Reading (NLT)

**Luke 15:11-32**

> <sup class="verse-num">11</sup> To illustrate the point further, Jesus told them this story: A man had two sons. <sup class="verse-num">12</sup> The younger son told his father, ‘I want my share of your estate now before you die.' So his father agreed to divide his wealth between his sons. <sup class="verse-num">13</sup> A few days later this younger son packed all his belongings and moved to a distant land, and there he wasted all his money in wild living. <sup class="verse-num">14</sup> About the time his money ran out, a great famine swept over the land, and he began to starve. <sup class="verse-num">15</sup> He persuaded a local farmer to hire him, and the man sent him into his fields to feed the pigs. <sup class="verse-num">16</sup> The young man became so hungry that even the pods he was feeding the pigs looked good to him. Please take me on as a hired servant.' <sup class="verse-num">20</sup> So he returned home to his father. And while he was still a long way off, his father saw him coming. Filled with love and compassion, he ran to his son, embraced him, and kissed him. <sup class="verse-num">21</sup> His son said to him, ‘Father, I have sinned against both heaven and you, and I am no longer worthy of being called your son.' <sup class="verse-num">22</sup> But his father said to the servants, ‘Quick! Bring the finest robe in the house and put it on him. Get a ring for his finger and sandals for his feet. <sup class="verse-num">23</sup> And kill the calf we have been fattening. So the party began. <sup class="verse-num">25</sup> Meanwhile, the older son was in the fields working. When he returned home, he heard music and dancing in the house, <sup class="verse-num">26</sup> and he asked one of the servants what was going on. <sup class="verse-num">27</sup> ‘Your brother is back,' he was told, ‘and your father has killed the fattened calf. For your brother was dead and has come back to life! He was lost, but now he is found!'

## The Story

Jesus shares a story about a family fractured by two very different kinds of rebellion. One son demands his inheritance early, effectively wishing his father dead, and squanders it in a distant country until he hits absolute rock bottom. The other son stays home, dutifully working the fields, but harbors a quiet, brewing resentment, viewing his relationship with his father as a ledger of debts and payments. When the younger son finally drags himself home, expecting rejection, the father abandons all cultural dignity to run out and embrace him. Yet the story doesn't end with that reunion; the father also leaves the ensuing celebration to seek out his rigid, furious older son in the dark.

## Application for Today

We often locate ourselves in one of these two brothers. Sometimes we run as far as we can, convinced our mistakes have permanently disqualified us from love and belonging. Other times, we stay close, but we treat our faith like a contract, believing our good behavior earns us a superior place. The shocking center of this story isn't the behavior of either son, but the relentless, undignified pursuit of the father. He does not wait for the wayward son to grovel, nor does he dismiss the angry son’s bitterness. He moves toward both of them, offering grace that cannot be earned or exhausted, reminding us that we are always welcome at his table.
