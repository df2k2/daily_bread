## The Reading (NLT)

**1 Corinthians 13**

> <sup class="verse-num">1</sup> If I could speak all the languages of earth and of angels, but didn't love others, I would only be a noisy gong or a clanging cymbal. <sup class="verse-num">2</sup> If I had the gift of prophecy, and if I understood all of God's secret plans and possessed all knowledge, and if I had such faith that I could move mountains, but didn't love others, I would be nothing. <sup class="verse-num">3</sup> If I gave everything I have to the poor and even sacrificed my body, I could boast about it; but if I didn't love others, I would have gained nothing. <sup class="verse-num">4</sup> Love is patient and kind. All that I know now is partial and incomplete, but then I will know everything completely, just as God now knows me completely. <sup class="verse-num">13</sup> Three things will last forever faith, hope, and love and the greatest of these is love.

## The Story

The early church in Corinth was a bustling, competitive community where people often treated spiritual abilities as status symbols. They argued over who had the most impressive gifts, who spoke the most languages, and who possessed the deepest theological knowledge. Paul writes this letter to disrupt their entire value system. He explains that even the most spectacular displays of faith or generosity are entirely hollow if they lack genuine care for others. In doing so, he shifts the focus from what people can achieve to how they treat one another, contrasting temporary human accomplishments with the eternal nature of divine affection.

## Application for Today

We still live in a world that rewards loud accomplishments and visible success. It is easy to measure our spiritual or personal growth by what we know, what we give, or how we perform in front of others. Yet this letter strips away those metrics, asking us to evaluate our lives by the quiet ways we support and honor those around us. True maturity means letting go of the need to be right, the urge to keep score, and the desire for recognition. When our current knowledge fades and our achievements are forgotten, the grace we extended to others is what will endure. We are invited to build our lives on the single foundation that outlasts time.
