## The Reading (NLT)

**1 John 4:7-16**

> <sup class="verse-num">7</sup> Dear friends, let us continue to love one another, for love comes from God. Anyone who loves is a child of God and knows God. <sup class="verse-num">8</sup> But anyone who does not love does not know God, for God is love. <sup class="verse-num">9</sup> God showed how much he loved us by sending his one and only Son into the world so that we might have eternal life through him. <sup class="verse-num">10</sup> This is real love not that we loved God, but that he loved us and sent his Son as a sacrifice to take away our sins. <sup class="verse-num">11</sup> Dear friends, since God loved us that much, we surely ought to love each other. <sup class="verse-num">12</sup> No one has ever seen God. But if we love each other, God lives in us, and his love is brought to full expression in us. <sup class="verse-num">13</sup> And God has given us his Spirit as proof that we live in him and he in us. <sup class="verse-num">14</sup> Furthermore, we have seen with our own eyes and now testify that the Father sent his Son to be the Savior of the world. <sup class="verse-num">15</sup> All who declare that Jesus is the Son of God have God living in them, and they live in God. <sup class="verse-num">16</sup> We know how much God loves us, and we have put our trust in his love. God is love, and all who live in love live in God, and God lives in them.

## The Story

The author of this letter writes to a community trying to figure out what authentic faith looks like in practice. He boils down their complex theological debates into a stark, inescapable reality about the nature of the Divine. Instead of pointing to mystical experiences or rigid rule-keeping, the writer insists that the very essence of the Creator is love. This was not presented as an abstract concept, but as a historical action demonstrated through the giving of the Son. The early Christians were taught that they could never separate their spiritual devotion from their social reality. To claim a relationship with the Divine meant their daily interactions had to reflect that same self-giving nature.

## Application for Today

It is easy to treat faith as a private matter of personal belief or intellectual agreement. Yet this passage challenges us to see our relationships as the primary arena where the sacred becomes visible. When we choose patience over snapping at a coworker, or generosity over self-interest, we are doing more than just being kind. We are actually giving flesh and bone to a reality that cannot be seen with the human eye. Our ordinary acts of compassion become the tangible proof that the Spirit is at work in the world. We are invited to let the affection we have received flow outward, transforming our communities one interaction at a time.
