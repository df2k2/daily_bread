## The Reading (NLT)

**2 Corinthians 4:16-18**

> <sup class="verse-num">16</sup> That is why we never give up. Though our bodies are dying, our spirits are being renewed every day. <sup class="verse-num">17</sup> For our present troubles are small and won't last very long. Yet they produce for us a glory that vastly outweighs them and will last forever! <sup class="verse-num">18</sup> So we don't look at the troubles we can see now; rather, we fix our gaze on things that cannot be seen. For the things we see now will soon be gone, but the things we cannot see will last forever.

## The Story

The author of this letter knew physical exhaustion intimately. Writing to a bustling, status-obsessed community in Corinth, he contrasts their culture of outward success with his own reality of beatings, shipwrecks, and daily weariness. Yet, he frames this relentless physical and emotional toll as something remarkably small. He introduces a radical shift in perspective, suggesting that external decay is not the final word. Instead, he points to a hidden, ongoing process of restoration happening deep within. The visible struggles, however heavy they feel, are temporary, while the invisible transformation holds enduring weight.

## Application for Today

It is incredibly easy to let our current hardships dictate our entire reality. When health fades, relationships fracture, or daily stressors pile up, these visible troubles demand our full attention. This text invites us to practice a different kind of vision. It asks us to trust that our present pain is not meaningless, but is somehow making room for a profound, lasting resilience. We are encouraged to look past the immediate horizon of our struggles. By focusing on the quiet, unseen work of grace within us, we can find the strength to keep going, even when the outer world feels like it is falling apart.
