## The Reading (NLT)

**Proverbs 3:5-8**

> <sup class="verse-num">5</sup> Trust in the Lord with all your heart; do not depend on your own understanding. <sup class="verse-num">6</sup> Seek his will in all you do, and he will show you which path to take. <sup class="verse-num">7</sup> Don't be impressed with your own wisdom. Instead, fear the Lord and turn away from evil. <sup class="verse-num">8</sup> Then you will have healing for your body and strength for your bones.

## The Story

The book of Proverbs is framed as an intimate conversation between a parent and a child, passing down the hard-earned wisdom of generations. In the ancient Near East, survival often depended on practical knowledge, yet the sages of Israel insisted that human intellect had a hard ceiling. They observed that relying solely on personal intuition often led to disaster and arrogance. True wisdom required a profound humility, an acknowledgment that the human mind cannot grasp the full picture of reality. By surrendering the need to control and figure everything out, the ancients believed a person could align with the divine order. This alignment was not just a mental exercise but a holistic way of living that brought deep vitality to the entire person.

## Application for Today

We live in an era that prizes self-reliance and insists we must chart our own course through sheer willpower and intellect. When faced with complex decisions or uncertain futures, our default is to overthink, strategize, and rely heavily on our own logic. Yet, this relentless pressure to have all the answers often leaves us exhausted and anxious. The wisdom of this passage invites us to lay down the exhausting burden of having to know exactly how things will work out. Yielding our plans to a broader, divine perspective does not mean we stop thinking, but rather that we stop worrying. When we release our grip on our own cleverness and choose to honor God's direction, we often experience a profound physical and emotional relief.
