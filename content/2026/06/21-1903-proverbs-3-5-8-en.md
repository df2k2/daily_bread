## The Reading (NLT)

**Proverbs 3:5-8**

> <sup class="verse-num">5</sup> Trust in the Lord with all your heart; do not depend on your own understanding. <sup class="verse-num">6</sup> Seek his will in all you do, and he will show you which path to take. <sup class="verse-num">7</sup> Don't be impressed with your own wisdom. Instead, fear the Lord and turn away from evil. <sup class="verse-num">8</sup> Then you will have healing for your body and strength for your bones.

## The Story

The book of Proverbs was compiled to pass down accumulated wisdom from one generation to the next, offering practical guidance for a life well-lived. In the ancient Near East, survival often depended on sharp instincts, careful planning, and human intellect. Yet, the teachers of Israel recognized a profound paradox at the heart of faith: human logic, while valuable, is ultimately incomplete. They taught that true wisdom begins precisely where our own comprehension ends. The writers understood the powerful temptation to rely entirely on our own cleverness to navigate life's complexities. To revere God meant acknowledging the boundaries of human insight and choosing to lean on a foundation much larger than oneself.

## Application for Today

We spend so much of our lives trying to figure everything out. When faced with uncertainty, our default response is usually to analyze, plan, and control every possible outcome. But there is an exhausting fragility in believing we must hold all the answers. This wisdom tradition invites us into the deep relief of surrender. It asks us to bring our whole hearts into a posture of trust, actively seeking divine guidance rather than just our own intellect. We are invited to let go of our need to manage every detail and instead walk forward with open hands. In that quiet release of control, we often find a profound sense of inner vitality and peace.
