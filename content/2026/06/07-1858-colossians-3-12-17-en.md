## The Reading (NLT)

> <sup class="verse-num">12</sup> Since God chose you to be the holy people he loves, you must clothe yourselves with tenderhearted mercy, kindness, humility, gentleness, and patience. <sup class="verse-num">13</sup> Make allowance for each other's faults, and forgive anyone who offends you. ## The Story

The letter to the Colossians was written to a young community trying to figure out how to live together in a diverse and often difficult world. The author uses the metaphor of getting dressed to describe how they should prepare themselves for their shared life. In the ancient world, putting on a garment was a deliberate act, often signifying a person's role or readiness for a specific task. By urging these early believers to deliberately adopt a specific set of communal virtues, the text frames these qualities not merely as internal feelings but as intentional, outward actions. They are the practical gear required to navigate the friction of human relationships. The passage points to a supreme virtue that acts as the final layer, holding all the other pieces securely in place.

## Application for Today

When we step out into the world each day, we are entirely responsible for what we bring into our interactions with others. We cannot control the hazards we might face or the difficult behaviors of those we encounter along the way, but we can choose our own posture. Deciding to deliberately practice grace is like putting on protective gear for the soul, shielding us from the wear and tear of inevitable conflicts. It requires a conscious decision to make room for the flaws of our fellow travelers, recognizing that we also rely on their goodwill. Letting an inner tranquility guide our decisions helps us maintain our footing when the path gets rocky. Everything we do becomes an opportunity to express deep appreciation for the companionship and the journey itself.
