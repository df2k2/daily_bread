## The Reading (NLT)

**Ephesians 3:14-21**

> <sup class="verse-num">14</sup> When I think of all this, I fall to my knees and pray to the Father, <sup class="verse-num">15</sup> the Creator of everything in heaven and on earth. <sup class="verse-num">16</sup> I pray that from his glorious, unlimited resources he will empower you with inner strength through his Spirit. <sup class="verse-num">17</sup> Then Christ will make his home in your hearts as you trust in him. Your roots will grow down into God's love and keep you strong. <sup class="verse-num">18</sup> And may you have the power to understand, as all God's people should, how wide, how long, how high, and how deep his love is. <sup class="verse-num">19</sup> May you experience the love of Christ, though it is too great to understand fully. Then you will be made complete with all the fullness of life and power that comes from God. <sup class="verse-num">20</sup> Now all glory to God, who is able, through his mighty power at work within us, to accomplish infinitely more than we might ask or think. <sup class="verse-num">21</sup> Glory to him in the church and in Christ Jesus through all generations forever and ever! Amen.

## The Story

The author of this letter pauses his theological instruction to adopt a posture of deep reverence, shifting from teaching to praying. He writes to a community living in a diverse, bustling cultural hub where people sought security in status, wealth, and local deities. Instead of asking for their physical safety or material success, he prays for their internal architecture. He envisions them like ancient trees with deep roots or grand buildings with unshakeable foundations, drawing their strength entirely from a divine source. This prayer pushes the boundaries of human language, trying to measure something fundamentally immeasurable. It describes a love with physical dimensions yet acknowledges it surpasses human comprehension.

## Application for Today

We often try to measure our worth by what we can quantify, like our achievements, our bank accounts, or the approval of others. Yet this passage invites us to find our grounding in a love that breaks all our measuring sticks. When life feels unstable or when our own resources run dry, we are reminded that our inner strength does not depend on our own resilience. It is fueled by an inexhaustible, expansive love that sustains us from the inside out. We are invited to simply rest in that vastness, allowing it to take up residence in our daily lives. As we trust in this endless provision, we find that our capacity to love and live fully expands far beyond our own imagination.
