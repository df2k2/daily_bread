## The Reading (NLT)

**Ephesians 3:14-21**

> <sup class="verse-num">14</sup> When I think of all this, I fall to my knees and pray to the Father, <sup class="verse-num">15</sup> the Creator of everything in heaven and on earth. <sup class="verse-num">16</sup> I pray that from his glorious, unlimited resources he will empower you with inner strength through his Spirit. <sup class="verse-num">17</sup> Then Christ will make his home in your hearts as you trust in him. Your roots will grow down into God's love and keep you strong. <sup class="verse-num">18</sup> And may you have the power to understand, as all God's people should, how wide, how long, how high, and how deep his love is. <sup class="verse-num">19</sup> May you experience the love of Christ, though it is too great to understand fully. Then you will be made complete with all the fullness of life and power that comes from God. <sup class="verse-num">20</sup> Now all glory to God, who is able, through his mighty power at work within us, to accomplish infinitely more than we might ask or think. <sup class="verse-num">21</sup> Glory to him in the church and in Christ Jesus through all generations forever and ever! Amen.

## The Story

The author writes this letter from a place of physical confinement, yet his mind travels to the cosmic scale of divine affection. He uses the language of both botany and architecture, imagining people growing deep roots like ancient trees and being built on unshakeable foundations. In the ancient world, people often felt at the mercy of chaotic spiritual forces and unpredictable fates. Against that backdrop of vulnerability, this prayer offers a radical alternative: a security that begins on the inside. The text envisions a love so expansive that it cannot be fully mapped or exhausted, only experienced.

## Application for Today

We spend so much energy trying to secure our external circumstances, hoping that safety out there will finally bring us peace in here. But true resilience moves in the opposite direction. When we allow ourselves to be grounded in the staggering reality of divine love, our inner world is transformed. We stop living from a place of scarcity and begin operating from a deep, quiet strength. This does not mean our external challenges simply vanish. Rather, it means that whatever terrain we are navigating, we are anchored in a reality far larger than our immediate fears.
