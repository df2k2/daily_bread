## The Reading (NLT)

**1 Peter 5:6-11**

> <sup class="verse-num">6</sup> So humble yourselves under the mighty power of God, and at the right time he will lift you up in honor. <sup class="verse-num">7</sup> Give all your worries and cares to God, for he cares about you. <sup class="verse-num">8</sup> Stay alert! Watch out for your great enemy, the devil. He prowls around like a roaring lion, looking for someone to devour. <sup class="verse-num">9</sup> Stand firm against him, and be strong in your faith. Remember that your family of believers all over the world is going through the same kind of suffering you are. <sup class="verse-num">10</sup> In his kindness God called you to share in his eternal glory by means of Christ Jesus. So after you have suffered a little while, he will restore, support, and strengthen you, and he will place you on a firm foundation. <sup class="verse-num">11</sup> All power to him forever! Amen.

## The Story

The early Christian communities scattered across Asia Minor were exhausted. They lived as outsiders, facing intense social pressure, suspicion, and quiet persecution from their neighbors. In this letter, they receive an acknowledgment of their deep anxiety and the very real threats surrounding them. The writer uses the terrifying imagery of a prowling predator to validate the hostility they feel in their daily lives. Yet, they are reminded that their struggles are not isolated; they belong to a global family enduring the exact same trials. The message points away from self-reliance and toward a posture of yielding to a Creator who sees their exhaustion.

## Application for Today

When we feel surrounded by hostility or overwhelmed by our own anxieties, our instinct is often to fight harder or worry more. This passage offers a different rhythm, asking us to let go of our need for control and hand our deepest fears over to a God who actively cares for us. We are challenged to remain alert to the destructive forces of despair and division, but to do so from a place of grounded trust rather than frantic panic. It helps to remember that our seasons of hardship are shared by a wider community, connecting us to believers everywhere. Ultimately, the promise is not an immediate escape from difficulty, but a divine restoration that rebuilds us on an unshakable foundation.
