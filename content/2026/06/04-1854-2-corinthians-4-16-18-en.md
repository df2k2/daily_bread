## The Reading (NLT)

**2 Corinthians 4:16-18**

> <sup class="verse-num">16</sup> That is why we never give up. Though our bodies are dying, our spirits are being renewed every day. <sup class="verse-num">17</sup> For our present troubles are small and won't last very long. Yet they produce for us a glory that vastly outweighs them and will last forever! <sup class="verse-num">18</sup> So we don't look at the troubles we can see now; rather, we fix our gaze on things that cannot be seen. For the things we see now will soon be gone, but the things we cannot see will last forever.

## The Story

The apostle's relationship with the Corinthian community was fraught, and his own life was marked by extreme physical hardship and persecution. Writing from a place of profound exhaustion, he acknowledges the undeniable reality of bodily decline and circumstantial suffering. Yet, he introduces a radical shift in perspective. Instead of dwelling on the immediacy of his exhaustion, he points toward an enduring, invisible reality that is quietly taking shape. He frames human endurance not as a matter of mere willpower, but as a deliberate redirection of focus toward what outlasts our current struggles.

## Application for Today

It is incredibly easy to be consumed by what is right in front of us. Pain, aging, grief, and daily frustrations demand our attention and can easily convince us that they are the only true things in the world. We are invited to cultivate a different kind of vision. This practice does not mean pretending our struggles are an illusion, but rather refusing to give them the final word. By choosing to anchor our attention in the enduring realities of God's love and restoration, we find a quiet, daily sustenance. Even when the outer edges of our lives feel frayed, there is a steady, unseen core being built up within us.
