## The Reading (NLT)

**Luke 15:11-32**

Filled with love and compassion, he ran to his son, embraced him, and kissed him. <sup class="verse-num">21</sup> His son said to him, ‘Father, I have sinned against both heaven and you, and I am no longer worthy of being called your son.' <sup class="verse-num">22</sup> But his father said to the servants, ‘Quick! For your brother was dead and has come back to life! He was lost, but now he is found!'

## The Story

Jesus tells this famous story to an audience of religious leaders who are grumbling about his habit of eating with outcasts. He crafts a narrative about two brothers who are both, in their own ways, alienated from their father. The younger brother initiates a severe cultural insult by claiming his estate prematurely, eventually squandering it far from home until he is completely destitute. Driven by sheer desperation, he rehearses an apology to beg for a job as a hired hand. Yet, the climax of the story rests entirely on the father's shocking reaction. Before the son can even deliver his practiced speech, the patriarch abandons all cultural dignity, running down the road to embrace his ruined boy. Meanwhile, the older brother, who stayed home and followed all the rules, reveals his own deep alienation through bitter resentment of this unearned grace.

## Application for Today

We often locate ourselves in one of these two brothers. Sometimes we are the younger sibling, running from our source of life and chasing empty promises until we hit rock bottom. In those moments, we assume we must earn our way back into good graces through hard work and groveling. Other times, we are the older sibling, keeping score and feeling resentful when grace is handed out to those we deem unworthy. But the focal point of the narrative isn't the failure of the sons; it is the staggering generosity of the parent. God is not a reluctant employer waiting for us to prove our worth, nor a strict taskmaster keeping a ledger of our good deeds. The divine response to our brokenness is to run out to meet us, offering a joyous welcome home.
