## The Reading (NLT)

**Psalm 1**

> <sup class="verse-num">1</sup> Oh, the joys of those who do not follow the advice of the wicked, or stand around with sinners, or join in with mockers. <sup class="verse-num">2</sup> But they delight in the law of the Lord, meditating on it day and night. <sup class="verse-num">3</sup> They are like trees planted along the riverbank, bearing fruit each season. Their leaves never wither, and they prosper in all they do. <sup class="verse-num">4</sup> But not the wicked! They are like worthless chaff, scattered by the wind. <sup class="verse-num">5</sup> They will be condemned at the time of judgment. Sinners will have no place among the godly. <sup class="verse-num">6</sup> For the Lord watches over the path of the godly, but the path of the wicked leads to destruction.

## The Story

As the gateway to the entire book of Psalms, this opening song acts as a preface for all the prayers, laments, and praises that follow. It presents a stark, ancient wisdom tradition: there are two distinct paths a life can take. In the agrarian world of ancient Israel, the contrast would have been instantly recognizable. A tree planted near an irrigation canal survives drought because its roots reach an unfailing water source. In contrast, the husks of threshed grain have no root and no weight, entirely at the mercy of the prevailing winds. The psalmist uses this everyday farming reality to illustrate the profound difference between a life anchored in divine instruction and one shaped by the cynicism of the crowd.

## Application for Today

It is easy to be swept up in the endless churn of public outrage and the cynical voices that dominate our culture. When we take our cues from the shifting winds of popular opinion, our inner lives become light, brittle, and easily scattered. The invitation here is to cultivate a different kind of resilience by drawing deeply from a quieter, steady source. Spiritual vitality is rarely instantaneous; it is the slow, often unseen work of sinking our roots into God's presence day after day. When we deliberately focus our attention on divine wisdom, we develop the capacity to weather harsh seasons. We may not always feel the growth happening, but over time, that hidden nourishment produces a life that holds firm and offers shade to others.
