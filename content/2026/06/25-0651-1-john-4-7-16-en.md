## The Reading (NLT)

**1 John 4:7-16**

> <sup class="verse-num">7</sup> Dear friends, let us continue to love one another, for love comes from God. Anyone who loves is a child of God and knows God. <sup class="verse-num">8</sup> But anyone who does not love does not know God, for God is love. <sup class="verse-num">9</sup> God showed how much he loved us by sending his one and only Son into the world so that we might have eternal life through him. <sup class="verse-num">10</sup> This is real love not that we loved God, but that he loved us and sent his Son as a sacrifice to take away our sins. <sup class="verse-num">11</sup> Dear friends, since God loved us that much, we surely ought to love each other. <sup class="verse-num">12</sup> No one has ever seen God. But if we love each other, God lives in us, and his love is brought to full expression in us. <sup class="verse-num">13</sup> And God has given us his Spirit as proof that we live in him and he in us. <sup class="verse-num">14</sup> Furthermore, we have seen with our own eyes and now testify that the Father sent his Son to be the Savior of the world. <sup class="verse-num">15</sup> All who declare that Jesus is the Son of God have God living in them, and they live in God. <sup class="verse-num">16</sup> We know how much God loves us, and we have put our trust in his love. God is love, and all who live in love live in God, and God lives in them.

## The Story

The early Christian communities receiving this letter were dealing with fractures and theological disputes. Some members claimed a special, hidden knowledge of the divine that elevated them above others. The author responds by stripping away the complexity and grounding the community's identity in something entirely visible and practical. Knowing the divine is not about possessing secret wisdom, but about participating in an active, self-giving affection. The writer insists that the ultimate reality of the universe is a relational presence that initiated connection with humanity. Through the life and sacrifice of Jesus, this presence demonstrated that true devotion is always outward-facing.

## Application for Today

We often look for spectacular signs or profound emotional experiences to confirm that we are connected to something greater than ourselves. Yet this passage suggests that the most profound evidence of the sacred in our lives is found in how we treat the people around us. When we choose patience over frustration or forgiveness over resentment, we are not merely being polite. We are actually participating in the very nature of the creator. We cannot see the source of all things with our physical eyes, but the invisible becomes visible every time we care for one another. Trusting in this foundational reality frees us from having to earn our worth, inviting us simply to receive that original affection and let it flow outward.
