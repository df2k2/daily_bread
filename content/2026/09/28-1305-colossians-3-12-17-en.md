## The Reading (NLT)

**Colossians 3:12-17**

> <sup class="verse-num">12</sup> Since God chose you to be the holy people he loves, you must clothe yourselves with tenderhearted mercy, kindness, humility, gentleness, and patience. <sup class="verse-num">13</sup> Make allowance for each other's faults, and forgive anyone who offends you. Remember, the Lord forgave you, so you must forgive others. <sup class="verse-num">14</sup> Above all, clothe yourselves with love, which binds us all together in perfect harmony. <sup class="verse-num">15</sup> And let the peace that comes from Christ rule in your hearts. For as members of one body you are called to live in peace. And always be thankful. <sup class="verse-num">16</sup> Let the message about Christ, in all its richness, fill your lives. Teach and counsel each other with all the wisdom he gives. Sing psalms and hymns and spiritual songs to God with thankful hearts. <sup class="verse-num">17</sup> And whatever you do or say, do it as a representative of the Lord Jesus, giving thanks through him to God the Father.

## The Story

Paul wrote to the church in Colossae to help them navigate the practical realities of their new faith. He used the metaphor of getting dressed to describe how they should interact with one another. In the ancient world, clothing indicated status, identity, and allegiance. By urging them to adopt specific virtues, he was asking them to deliberately embrace a new identity rooted in their status as people chosen and loved by God. These traits were not just individual moral achievements but essential tools for surviving and thriving together in a diverse, friction-filled community. The ultimate garment, tying everything together, was love.

## Application for Today

We often wake up and choose our physical clothes based on the weather or the tasks ahead of us. This passage invites us to be just as intentional about our spiritual posture before we interact with the world. True community is messy, and offenses are inevitable, which is why patience and forgiveness are presented as daily necessities rather than occasional heroic acts. When we choose to wrap ourselves in compassion and humility, we create space for peace to actually take root in our relationships. Every conversation and action becomes an opportunity to reflect the grace we have already received.
